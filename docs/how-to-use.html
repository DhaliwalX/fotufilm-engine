How to use Fotufilm

Fotufilm models the physical chain of analog photography—scene light, camera lens filters, emulsion layers, development, grain, and optical printing onto photographic paper or cinema release film.

Quick start: the four darkroom stages

Every Fotufilm host—desktop, browser, mobile, CLI, and video plug-in—runs your image through the same four physical stages:

  1. Film: Pick one of the 47 bundled film stocks (color negative, slide, black-and-white, instant, or motion picture), choose a physical film gauge (Super 8 through 4×5), and set film age or halation.
  2. Expose: Control the light entering the camera before the emulsion responds—adjust Exposure in stops, Highlights, Shadows, Camera Preflash, Source Illuminant, White Balance, optical Lens Filters, and the three-way Grade.
  3. Develop: Push or pull development using measured process curves, blend metallic silver with Bleach Bypass, and dial in calibrated Standard or crystal-level Film grain, mottle, and interlayer couplers.
  4. Print: Choose how the developed film is viewed or printed—Match Film selects RA-4 paper for still negatives, cinema print film for motion-picture stocks, or direct positive viewing for slide films—then add optional film or paper borders and export.

Tip: Instant before/after check. Hold Space on a keyboard, or press and hold the photograph on any touch screen, to compare your developed print with the original image.

Desktop & browser editor (Mac, Linux, Web)

Install the signed macOS package, run the Linux x86-64 AppImage, build from source with ./flutter/build.sh, or open fotufilm.com/demo in a WebGPU-capable browser. All four share the same three-column darkroom.

1. Open photos, RAW files, videos, or folders

  • Click + in the top-left toolbar (or press ⌘O / Ctrl+O, or drag files onto the window) to open camera RAWs, JPEGs, HEICs, 16-bit PNGs, 16/32-bit TIFFs, linear OpenEXR scenes, or video clips.
  • On desktop, opening a single file from Finder or File → Open… automatically populates the bottom filmstrip with neighboring photos and videos in the same folder so you can step through a shoot without leaving the canvas.
  • Click the Library button next to the wordmark (or press L) to link folders on disk, rate frames from 1 to 5 stars, filter by edited photos, and search across tens of thousands of files without copying or uploading anything.

2. Choose a stock and inspect the live tone curve

  • Filter the left sidebar by All, Color Negative, Slide, B&W, or Motion Picture, or type in the search box. Visible tiles render live thumbnails of your open photo.
  • Watch the RGB tone curve and histogram at the top of the right-hand Darkroom panel: it plots how input values from 0 to 255 travel through the film, output paper, and grade in real time.
  • Switch to the Presets tab at the top of the left sidebar to save your current darkroom recipe or apply a saved preset to new frames.

3. Selective adjustments, crop, and batch export

  • Auto Adjust (⌘⇧A / Ctrl+Shift+A): Solves Exposure, Highlights, and Shadows from regional scene brightness and the active film’s latitude.
  • Selective (⌘5) & Crop (⌘6 or C): Use toolbar canvas tools above the image to make targeted Color, Light, or Subject (macOS) adjustments, or straighten perspective with independent four-corner cropping.
  • Copy, Paste & Export All: Copy settings from one frame and paste them onto others, then export a single frame (⌘S / Ctrl+S) or batch-export every open photo into a folder as JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, 16-bit Display P3 TIFF, or 10-bit Cineon Log DPX.

Compact & Android editor

On Android phones and narrow browser windows, the editor switches to a dark, thumb-friendly layout that keeps your photograph unobstructed in the center.

  1. Import into Photos or Negatives: Tap + in the top-right corner of the home gallery to import photos, RAWs, or scanned negatives.
  2. Pick a tool from the bottom rail: Tap Film Strip, Grain, Lens, Print, Light & Color, Select, or Crop to open its sheet beneath the canvas, and drag the sheet handle to resize or dismiss it.
  3. Fine-tune sliders anywhere on the track: Drag horizontally anywhere along a slider row—the value moves smoothly from your finger’s starting point and pauses gently on its default value as you pass it. Double-tap any slider to reset it.

Converting scanned negatives

Already shoot 35mm, 120, or 4×5 film? Fotufilm can invert linear or camera-RAW scans of developed C-41 and black-and-white negatives and print them through its optical paper and minilab models:

  1. Import as a negative: Choose File → Import Scanned Negative… on desktop, or click + → Negative in the browser and mobile editors.
  2. Set film base and scan hardware: Fotufilm estimates the orange mask automatically and suggests matching negative stocks. Click Pick Clear Film on an unexposed border or sprocket strip for an exact base reading, then set Scanned With (camera RAW or dedicated scanner) and Scanned Under (White LED, Phone/Tablet Screen, Halogen, or RGB LEDs).
  3. Balance a whole roll: Link a folder of negative scans in the Library and click Measure Roll in the Roll tab so every frame shares consistent roll-wide highlight balance and channel contrast.
  4. Straighten borders & print: Drag the four independent corners in Crop to trim the film holder, then choose an RA-4 paper, Lab Scan, or Digital Reference in Print.

Command-line tool (fotufilm)

Build and run the Swift CLI directly from the repository root to batch-develop images, test optical printer setups, or benchmark GPU vs. CPU throughput.

# Build the release CLI
swift build -c release --product fotufilm

# Develop a photograph on Portra 400 printed onto Kodak Endura Premier paper
.build/release/fotufilm input.dng output.tiff --stock portra400 --paper endura-premier

# Develop a motion-picture stock onto Kodak Vision 2383 with custom printer lights
.build/release/fotufilm frame.exr print.png --stock vision500t --paper vision-2383 \
  --printer-red 28 --printer-green 25 --printer-blue 23

# Print a slide onto Ilfochrome/Cibachrome positive paper
.build/release/fotufilm slide-scene.jpg print.jpg --stock velvia50 --paper cibachrome

# Benchmark 30 warm renders on Metal vs CPU
.build/release/fotufilm input.jpg output.jpg --stock cinestill800t --iterations 31

DaVinci Resolve & Final Cut Pro plug-ins

The macOS installer (and ./resolve/build.sh / ./finalcut/build.sh) installs native Metal-accelerated plug-ins that run the exact same scene-linear Rec.2020 film pipeline inside your NLE.

DaVinci Resolve (OpenFX)

  1. Drop Fotufilm onto a clip or timeline node in the Color page.
  2. Choose your color-management mode:
    • Fotufilm mode (fastest for camera log in unmanaged DaVinci YRGB): Set Color Management to Fotufilm, pick your camera’s recording log under Input Color Space (such as Apple Log / Rec.2020, S-Log3 / S-Gamut3.Cine, F-Log2, or V-Log), and set Output Color Space to match your display/delivery target (such as Rec.709 / Gamma 2.4). No surrounding CST nodes are required.
    • Timeline mode (for DaVinci Wide Gamut or ACES pipelines): Keep Color Management on Timeline and match Timeline Color Space to the working space entering the node (for example, DaVinci Wide Gamut / Intermediate or ACEScct).
  3. Avoid double tone-mapping: A finished Fotufilm paper or cinema print is already display-referred—do not place an additional output tone map after a finished print node unless you intentionally set Stage to Negative Only or Texture Only.

Final Cut Pro & Motion (FxPlug)

  1. Use a Wide Gamut HDR library whenever grading Apple Log or HDR clips so highlight headroom above diffuse white reaches the emulsion intact for halation and shoulder roll-off.
  2. Apply Fotufilm from the Effects browser, and place any basic clip exposure or white-balance trim above Fotufilm in the Video inspector so adjustments happen before film exposure.
