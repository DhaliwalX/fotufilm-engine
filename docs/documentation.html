Fotufilm guide

Try the browser editor to develop photos and videos locally in your browser, select films and output media, adjust exposure and inspect the pipeline. Large photos use overlapping tiles that include the print blur to keep tile boundaries seamless.

Fotufilm adds a film look to photos and videos. This repository includes the engine, command-line tool, Mac app, Resolve and Final Cut plugins, and browser demo.

Build and open the app

Install Xcode 26 or newer and Halide. Run swift build to build the engine and swift test -c release --parallel to test it. Run macos/build.sh --test to build the Mac app.

Open build/macos/Fotufilm.app. Choose File → Open…, select a photo or video, and choose a film.

The Mac darkroom

The film library sits to the left of the photograph. The Darkroom panel on the right follows four stages, with the current stage highlighted in the tabs. You can move between them at any time; each adjustment updates the preview.

Small ? help buttons appear after control and section labels. Hover to read a tooltip, or click to open the explanation. Descriptions follow the selected film and options. Short status messages remain visible in the panel.

Choose Fotufilm → Settings… (⌘,) to open the separate Mac settings window. General contains defaults for new photos and film suggestions. Output groups photo, video, and negative viewing options. Film Model groups grain, halation, and color separation defaults. Changes apply immediately, and the window can stay open while you edit. Use the window’s close button or ⌘W when finished.

  1. Film: choose a stock in the library, then set its film format, age, and halation.
  2. Expose: adjust exposure, highlights, shadows, and colour balance, then use Grade directly below. Its Shadows, Midtones, and Highlights tabs show one colour pad and level slider at a time, while retaining each band’s adjustments. The light controls act before the film responds; Grade’s lift, gamma, and gain act on the developed image. Lens filters, source interpretation, video encoding, lens correction, and supported long-exposure settings also live here.
  3. Develop: set push/pull and bleach bypass, then tune grain, mottle, and the stock’s colour-separation controls.
  4. Print: choose the output medium, enlarger, and viewing light where supported. Video cadence is also here. Save with the panel’s Export button, File → Export…, or the toolbar’s Export button.

Selective and Crop are canvas tools in the toolbar above the photograph, beside the zoom controls. They are available for still photos and open their settings in the right-hand panel. Choose a darkroom stage to return to its controls. Use ⌘1–⌘4 for the four stages, ⌘5 for Selective, and ⌘6 for Crop, or choose them from the View menu. Click and hold the photograph to compare with the original. With Normal selected, Expose and Print remain available. Film and Develop show that film simulation is off; choose a film from the library to enable their controls.

Selective light and colour adjustments are saved with the photo, remain visible after closing the tool, and are included in photo exports. Undo and Redo include selective changes. Subject masks follow crop and rotation. Show Mask is a temporary preview; it is never exported.

Film packs

The Mac film sidebar offers Load custom pack…. Film Workshop is temporarily hidden on Mac and iPad, including its menu and keyboard shortcut. The pack purchase button is hidden.

Packs loaded in the Mac app are also available in the Fotufilm plugins on the same Mac and user account. Restart Resolve or Final Cut Pro after loading or updating a pack. Install matching app and plugin versions; older plugins skip packs that require a newer release.

The app and plugins include all 40 film profiles for free, including in default source builds. No activation is needed.

Thirty-three film profiles, including Gold 200, use smooth cubic interpolation through every validated digitized point from their source characteristic curves. This preserves small variations in the traced data and removes the old analytic clamp kinks. Points match within floating-point precision; they are not a claim of exact physical calibration. Beyond the source graph's exposure range, the response is extrapolated to constant density limits.

The native Metal camera renderer caches the cubic coefficients while preserving every sampled point. See the iPad performance measurements for timings and device limits.

These sampled profiles use stock schema version 2 and require an engine, app or plugin built with sampled-curve support. Older builds reject them. Existing schema version 1 profiles remain supported.

Scene light

Source Illuminant defaults to Stock Native for RAW photos, processed photos and video. It follows the selected film's reference light: 3200 K tungsten for 500T, for example. This is a stock-balanced emulation assumption, not a reconstruction of the original scene illumination.

In the Mac editor and plugins, choose a daylight or tungsten preset, or Custom and Source Illuminant (K) (1000–25000 K), to expose the film under a different source. A daylight source on a tungsten stock retains the lighting mismatch. Warmth and Tint refine the selected light; warmer light means lower Kelvin and positive tint adds green light. Source settings do not change the printing head or Viewing Illuminant.

Output calibration stays at the stock's reference light. Extreme tint shifts are limited to physically representable light.

RAW imports retain their complete as-shot white for decoding and camera-profile correction. Capture metadata no longer automatically selects the film-exposure illuminant. Changing Source Illuminant, Warmth or Tint does not re-demosaic the image. A Kelvin value assumes a smooth Planckian/daylight spectrum; it does not identify an LED or fluorescent lamp.

The CLI --scene-kelvin 3200 explicitly chooses tungsten light; omitting it uses Stock Native. --wb is a relative mired adjustment to that source on RAW and processed inputs alike: 6504 leaves it unchanged. --tint changes its green/magenta balance. Plugins expect an already white-balanced input. Older RAW edits that relied on automatic capture-light simulation can render differently; select the former capture temperature to reproduce that assumption. Explicitly stored camera-light overrides in saved app edits are retained as Custom.

Developers can supply an exact capture xy white or an 81-sample spectrum covering 380–780 nm at 5 nm. A supplied spectrum takes precedence over the temperature/tint controls. Relative spectra are normalized to equal luminance, including lamps with no energy at 560 nm.

Print models

The output menu names each paper or print film: Kodak Ektacolor Edge, Kodak Professional Endura Premier, Fujicolor Crystal Archive Type CA, Kodak Vision 2383, Kodak Vision Premier 2393, and Fujifilm ETERNA-CP 3513DI. Each is digitised from the manufacturer's own published datasheet: Kodak E-7020, E-4070 and H-1-2383, Fujifilm AF3-0250U2, and the 2393 and ETERNA-CP 3513DI sheets. Dye spectra, layer sensitivities and characteristic curves are read from those publications rather than modelled. The film strip offers photo prints for still gauges and projection prints plus Telecine for motion-picture gauges; the film name does not change the choices. The CLI and tests also include synthetic films and a generated colour chart.

Release prints time a correctly exposed grey card to approximately 1.0 density above clear film (10% transmission), regardless of the camera stock. Photo paper retains its 18% grey target. This makes release-print midtones darker and gives highlights more room than the previous paper-grey timing. Kodak 2383 now develops red, green and blue along separate curves from its datasheet. Existing stock-pack paperMidDensity values still load, but the selected output medium sets the grey target.

Release printing uses an approximate UV-blocked tungsten RGB additive printer, separate from the xenon light used to view the print. Complete measured printer-filter spectra are unavailable in the source sheets, so the passbands are approximate. Fujifilm ETERNA-CP's published Gray curve does not agree with its dye sum under a non-negative additive-base model; the published dyes are retained, and their neutral spectral calibration remains uncertain.

The CLI IDs are ektacolor-edge, endura-premier, crystal-archive, vision-2383, vision-2393, and eterna-cp. These identifiers are preserved, so saved print selections continue to resolve. Existing complete colour grades still load; incomplete grades are rejected.

The Enlarger menu, offered where a negative is optically enlarged onto a reflection paper, chooses the lamp house. Diffuser is the default and reads the negative at the diffuse density the film sheets are measured in, so it changes nothing. Condenser applies the Callier effect: collimated light loses what the image scatters, so densities read at their specular values, about 1.4 times diffuse for a silver black-and-white negative and about 1.05 times for a dye negative. Each material carries its own figure, so Bleach Bypass matters here: the silver the bleach leaves behind scatters like a black-and-white negative and reads at the silver figure, and the more of it a colour negative retains, the more the condenser moves it. The print is re-timed through the scaled mid-grey, so the head shows as harder contrast rather than a darker print, and a normally processed colour negative barely moves. Scans, the negative view, Digital Reference, cinema release prints and reversal film have no enlarger in their path and ignore the menu. The CLI flag is --enlarger diffuser|condenser.

Halation models

Halation Model offers Legacy and Layered Transport. Find it under Film Model in Mac and iOS settings, in the Halation group in Resolve and Final Cut, and under Film → Character in the browser. Legacy is the default for existing settings and projects. Layered Transport traces wavelength-dependent light through the film stack. It uses an inferred stock construction where included, otherwise an illustrative stack. These constructions are estimates, not measured film stacks. Estimated Halation Shape applies only to Legacy.

Layered Transport is experimental, uses more memory and may render more slowly. Both models use the same capture illuminant; when a photo has no capture-light metadata, both use the film’s reference light to preserve neutral balance. Donor-layer stocks currently require Legacy. Browser transport uses SIMD WebAssembly and develops the whole frame; the browser uses the film’s default output medium, and stage inspection remains available with Legacy. RAW photos with a detected capture light require Legacy because the browser transport data is calibrated for the film’s reference light. See the model guide for the illustrative A/B, construction fields, and backend limits.

The CLI supports --transport-backend cpu|metal. Reduced bands use smooth cubic B-spline reconstruction across camera and editor paths. Use --iterations 31 to measure 30 warm renders, including median and p95 frame times and achieved throughput; image decoding and output encoding are excluded from warm timings.

Library rendering

The Swift package includes the shader resources needed by FotufilmMetal. Package consumers can initialize renderers outside the source checkout without setting a shader search path.

When reusing an edit key, HandwrittenMetalFullFrameRenderer.prepareChecked keeps the previous edit usable if preparation fails. Frames already queued continue with the settings they were encoded with. Calling removeAll() gives the next edit a fresh frame pool, so queued frames from the previous edit do not occupy its preview slots.

Use FilmEngineInvocation(validating:options:width:height:) or the engine’s processChecked, developNegativeChecked and printPositiveChecked methods to handle unsupported push/pull settings as FilmDevelopmentError. Checked Metal preparation propagates the same error; Boolean preparation methods return false. The original nonthrowing entry points require known-valid settings and report the development error if that requirement is violated.

HalideMetalFilmRenderer.minimumPeakBytes returns an optional estimate. Unwrap it before calculating a memory budget; nil means the requested development condition cannot be prepared. canRender returns false in that case.

Scanned negatives

Choose File → Import Scanned Negative… and open a RAW or unconverted image with its clear film border visible. Drag over an unexposed border patch, choose the closest film, and use Preview Positive. Import Positive opens the result with Normal selected and the Crop tool ready.

Drag the four crop corners independently, then leave Crop or press Return to straighten the selected area. Corner edits support undo, saved edits and full-resolution export. Rotation and flipping keep the four-corner selection attached to the image. Drags use a cached display preview; film thumbnails refresh after leaving Crop. Choose an aspect ratio for a rectangular crop, or Reset Crop to start again.

Conversion is approximate and does not require a calibration wedge. Use unadjusted scans and their file colour profile; select Linear Samples only for a known linear scan. The positive retains full resolution in a 16-bit TIFF. Reimport the original to change border sampling or film choice. See the input requirements and conversion limits.

Developers can convert linear negative scans with the scanned-negative library API. Supply dark and light references plus a calibration mapping for the capture setup and film. The API recovers film densities and enters the existing print stage, preserving halation and grain already in the film. Measured scanner profiles and CLI controls are not included.

Browser editor

The CPU and WebGPU film renderers preserve the same scene-linear float32 response for identical pixels, settings and film packs. Display conversion and 8-bit sRGB export happen after film processing.

The browser editor is experimental, as marked in its toolbar. It uses the Mac app’s film sidebar, photo canvas, and adjustment inspector, with a neutral theme. Open one or several photos or videos, or drag them onto the canvas. Select open files in the strip below the canvas; each keeps its own adjustments and undo history for the session. Processing stays on your device.

Import progress names the current RAW operation, such as unpacking sensor data, applying camera white balance, and demosaicing. Any percentage applies to that operation. Preview and export progress identifies profile loading, shader compilation, local tone measurement, render tiles, and image encoding. Tile counts report the current frame, including its output size.

While a preview is queued, the status keeps the running operation visible and shows the latest waiting edit after “Next”, including its value and preview size. New edits replace the waiting preview. If the engine is occupied by a film thumbnail or another render, the status names that work and follows its current operation.

Use the sidebar’s search and film list to choose a profile, or choose Normal to edit without film. Visible film thumbnails develop the open photo. Adjustments update while you drag. Preview resolution adapts to rendering speed, then returns to up to 1600 pixels on its long edge when you stop. Zooming requests more detail from the original. Film uses Halide on WebGPU when the browser supports its required features, and otherwise uses WebAssembly SIMD. Normal can use the scene-linear JavaScript reference if the WASM runtime cannot start.

  • Film: Grain, New Grain Pattern, and Output Medium. Choose Digital Reference, a photo paper or projection print film, Lab Scan, Telecine, or Negative. Reversal films use Digital Reference. The selection updates the preview and export, and is included in undo and saved edits. Physical receivers use the selected engine paper or print-film profile.
  • Light & Color: Exposure, Highlights, Shadows, Regional, Temperature, Tint, Saturation, Vibrance, and a three-way grade. Regional keys highlight and shadow changes to surrounding tones. Encoded Grade applies the grade in the encoded signal.
  • Crop: four independent corners, aspect-ratio presets, Rotate Left, Flip, and Straighten. Use Preview Crop to see the applied frame and Edit Corners to adjust the selection. Straighten switches to the applied preview and updates while dragging. Done or Return closes the crop inspector. The same geometry is used for export.
  • The toolbar offers panel toggles, zoom, histogram, undo, redo, reset, and export. Hold the photo or Space to compare with the original. Zoom remains in place as adjustments update.
  • Options → Save edits… saves a browser edit file; Load edits… restores it onto the current photo. The file stores settings, not image data. Browser edit files are separate from Mac documents.
  • Options → Inspect pipeline shows intermediate stages and amplified differences. Export always develops the finished image.

Export develops the original image at full resolution or the chosen maximum long edge, with the current crop and adjustments. PNG, JPEG and WebP use 8-bit sRGB; JPEG and WebP offer a Quality control. Display P3 output from the Mac app can retain saturated colors outside sRGB. For color comparisons, convert both images to the same color space; use a 16-bit Mac export to reduce rounding differences during conversion. Imports above 120 megapixels are rejected. Files and unsaved edits are cleared when the page closes.

Video: open MP4, MOV, M4V, WebM or MKV files when the browser supports their video codec. Use Play, Pause and the position slider below the preview. Set Trim in and Trim out in seconds. All film, output-medium, exposure, color, crop, rotate, flip and pipeline-inspection controls apply to the current frame; export applies the finished settings throughout the trim range. Preview playback can skip frames while the film engine is busy. Export renders every frame with its original timing, including variable frame rate, and varies the grain seed per frame.

Input color space offers the same choices as the Mac app: Standard, Apple Log / Rec.2020, Apple Log 2 / Apple Wide Gamut, S-Log3 / S-Gamut3.Cine, S-Log3 / S-Gamut3, S-Log2 / S-Gamut, F-Log / F-Gamut, F-Log2 / F-Gamut, F-Log2 C / F-Gamut C, and HLG / Rec.2020. Select the format used when recording; log encodings are not detected automatically. Standard follows supported SDR, HLG or PQ color tags. HEVC Main / Main10 4:2:0 uses a software decoder when native decoding cannot expose original pixels. Native decoded YUV planes retain 10- and 12-bit code values where exposed by the browser; curve and gamut conversion produces scene-linear Rec.2020 floats before film processing. Unsupported native pixel formats or color tags report an error instead of silently tone-mapping the input.

Export video offers MP4 / H.264 and WebM / VP9, full resolution or a smaller long edge, and Medium, High or Very high quality. Output is 8-bit SDR. The primary video track is exported; Include audio preserves available audio tracks with synchronized timing, copying compatible audio or transcoding when required. If audio cannot be preserved, export reports an error; choose another format or disable Include audio. Odd output dimensions are padded by one pixel for codec compatibility. Input color space, trimming and audio settings are included in undo and saved edits.

Videos stay on this device. Input is read from the selected file in bounded chunks; export encodes frames one at a time with backpressure and writes to a chosen disk file. Where the browser has no save picker, export uses browser-managed disk storage and provides a Download link when complete. Save that download before dismissing it or leaving the page. Cancel and failed exports discard their temporary output. There is no whole-file memory fallback or fixed input-file size limit; disk quota, browser codec support and available memory for an individual frame still apply. Video frames above 40 megapixels are rejected. Closing the page clears the current editing session.

Linear EXR: the demo opens an original float32 Rec.2020 scene with a 16-stop neutral ramp and bright sources up to 64. Open another flat, single-part RGB half-float or float32 EXR to use your own scene. EXR files are interpreted as linear; embedded chromaticities are converted to Rec.2020, with linear Rec.709 assumed when absent. Negative values and highlights above 1 survive import, crop, preview processing and film development. The image on screen is a display preview; it is never fed back into the film engine. Associated EXR color is composited over black. Deep, multipart and subsampled-channel EXRs are unsupported. Limits are 512 MB and 120 megapixels. Export remains 8-bit sRGB.

Camera RAW: open or drop DNG, CR2/CR3, NEF, ARW, RAF, ORF, RW2 and other LibRaw-supported files alongside ordinary photos. RAW decoding runs locally in a worker, applies the camera’s orientation and as-shot white balance, and blends clipped highlights to avoid false pink or purple colors. It stores linear Rec.2020 pixels at 16 bits with an exposure scale, restoring floating-point highlight headroom for adjustments, crop and export. Explicit DNG baseline exposure is honored. The toolbar marks these files RAW. Cancel stops an import without removing the current photo. Camera support depends on LibRaw 0.22.2; some compression modes and newer cameras are not supported. The Mac app uses Core Image, whose camera profiles, baseline exposure and highlight recovery can still produce different results. The Temperature control adjusts the decoded as-shot result. RAW imports are limited to 512 MB and 120 megapixels; available browser memory may impose a lower limit. Export remains 8-bit sRGB.

For cameras with a bundled measured spectral profile and usable RAW white-balance metadata, the browser applies the same temperature-dependent camera correction as the Mac app before adjustments and film rendering. It preserves floating-point highlights in previews, thumbnails and exports. Hover over the RAW readout to see the selected profile and estimated temperature. The estimate comes from RAW white-balance metadata and the decoder’s active camera matrix; it may differ from Core Image’s temperature. Unmatched cameras or missing white-balance metadata retain the RAW decoder’s color. Film rendering also integrates the capture light from RAW white-balance metadata, including cameras without a matching measured camera profile. Previews, film thumbnails, pipeline inspection and exports share that exposure table. Photos without usable capture-light metadata retain the film’s reference-light rendering. Decoder baseline exposure, estimated capture temperature and highlight recovery can still differ from the Mac app.

The browser editor does not yet match all Mac features. Scanned-negative conversion, lens filters and correction, selective adjustments, custom packs, film format changes, spectral halation and coupler controls, lab controls, and HDR or 16-bit exports remain Mac-only. The browser’s film kernels still render the halation, couplers, development, grain and output model carried by each exported profile.

Shortcuts: Command/Control-O opens images, Command/Control-S exports, Command/Control-Z undoes, Shift-Command/Control-Z redoes, H toggles the histogram, C opens Crop, + and − zoom, and 0 fits the photo. Double-click a slider to reset that adjustment.

Plugins

Run resolve/build.sh --test or finalcut/build.sh --test to test a plugin. Building the installable Final Cut plugin also requires Apple's FxPlug SDK. See the Resolve guide and Final Cut guide.

Reopening a saved Final Cut or Motion project retains its film format, output medium, lens choices and texture-stage controls.

Resolve controls

The inspector groups controls under Input, Film, Light & Colour, Lens & Filters, Development, Grain, Halation, Colour Separation, Output, and Pipeline, in the order the light passes through them. Input, Film, and Output start open. The Final Cut Pro plugin uses the same groups. Resolved Format, Resolved Medium, and Effective Renderer show the settings in use.

Timeline Color SpaceWhat this node is being handed — the one control that is not taste. The film model is scene-referred in linear Rec.2020: mid-grey at 0.18, specular highlights above 1.0. The input is decoded to that and the result encoded back, so a wrong setting shows the emulsion the wrong light and lands the characteristic curves whole stops off. Auto reads the space the host tags the clip with, where the host says. A display-referred space (Rec.709, sRGB) clips at diffuse white, leaving halation nothing bright to scatter; a wide-gamut log or linear timeline keeps the highlights the model was built for.

The image height maps onto the gauge's frame height, so a smaller format is enlarged more and shows coarser grain, wider halation and stronger adjacency — the same emulsion at a different magnification. Filter 1An absorbing filter on the front of the lens. It is integrated spectrally against the chosen film's own three layer sensitivities, which is why the same filter is a different filter on a different stock — an 85B is a correction on tungsten film and a heavy warm cast on daylight film. It also adds the veiling glare of two more air-glass faces, whatever the Veiling Glare slider says — and that added glare is why this control is live only on the Full stage: no kernel this build carries can measure it in a span that ends at the developed negative.
Filter 2A second filter, behind the first. They stack in the order given: their transmittances multiply, and the gap between them makes a ghost of its own.
Filter 3A third filter, behind the second.
MeteringHow the exposure was set with those filters fitted. Metered through is the camera's own photopic cell reading the light that got past the glass, and is the default; it can underexpose behind a narrow filter, because the meter does not use the film's sensitivity. Filter factor is the published compensation, worked out against the emulsion, which restores the luminance record without cancelling the colour change. None is a fixed manual exposure, so the light the filter took lands on the film as underexposure. Ignored with no filter fitted, and live only on the Full stage, which is the only span a filter is live in.
DiffusionA diffusion filter on the front of the lens. A share of the light meets a particle and leaves in a new direction, and the lens images it somewhere else on the frame; the share that missed every particle is untouched, which is why a diffused picture keeps its edges instead of going soft. The black families carry absorbing particles: the blacks still lift and the highlights bloom much less.
Diffusion GradeThe particle loading a product line's 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1 and 2 name: one formulation more heavily loaded, so the grade moves how much light takes part and never how far it goes. Ignored with no diffusion filter fitted.
Focal LengthThe taking lens's focal length in millimetres, read only by the diffusion filter: a ray deviated by an angle ahead of the lens lands focal length times that angle off its unscattered position, so the same filter glows bigger on a longer lens, exactly as it does in the world. 0 — the default — is the gauge's own normal lens, which is what the grade numbering on a filter's ring is calibrated around.
Veiling GlareVeiling glare from the taking lens, as a multiplier on the stock's figure. It defaults to 0 because a photographed clip already carries the glare of the lens that shot it, and this stage would veil the shadows a second time. Raise it for light that has met no glass — a render or a synthetic chart — or to stand in for glass worse than the camera's.
Filter CoatingCoating on every fitted absorbing and diffusion filter. Changes transmission and added glare. Multicoated preserves the existing filter look.
Push / PullMeasured push or pull conditions for this film's stated developer, dilution, temperature and agitation. The control is disabled when the stock pack has no measured response.
Bleach BypassHow much of the developed silver the bleach leaves in the negative. The retained silver is a black-and-white image over the colour one: contrast up, chroma down, together.
Film Age (years)Years the roll sat past its process-by date. Speed falls a stop a decade with the blue-sensitive layer going first, base fog rises, and grain rises with the fog — the muddy, crossed toe of an old roll.
Long Exposure (s)Exposure duration for the stock's measured reciprocity response. 0 disables the override. Corrections stop at the last measured duration; no motion blur is added. Use the same value in paired Negative Only and Print Only nodes.
GrainMultiplier on the stock's measured granularity. 0 disables it.
MottleUse the stock's coarse grain mixture, or set a custom share. Full mode only.
Mottle Amount (%)Share of grain variance carried by coarse clumping. Total calibrated granularity is preserved. Custom mottle uses the engine's video delivery size.
Grain AnimationTimeline changes grain each frame. Frozen uses the same field at every time; the texture still responds to changes in the image.
Grain ModelDisc grain is available on silver-image stocks and requires Reference rendering, selected automatically. Subpixel grain uses the clump field; coarse mottle is suppressed where discs render.
Grain SeedSame seed and same frame give the same grain. Grain also advances with the timeline, so a still frame is still, and a moving one is not.
Halation ModelLegacy or layered optical transport. Layered uses an inferred stock construction where included, otherwise an illustrative stack.
HalationMultiplier on the fraction of light the base returns. On the legacy model it scales the light going down rather than the finished halo, so raising it widens the halo as well as brightening it — the way a thinner antihalation layer would. With Estimated Halation Shape on, the film's geometry is pinned and this scales the amount alone. 1 is the stock's authored look — the sheet's look scale times the measured film, whose calibrated returns sit at the patent-floor absorber densities and are all but invisible on their own. The measured film survives at 1 over the look scale (0.025 on a rem-jet stock). Typing past the slider reaches 100.
Estimated Halation ShapeRenders halation through the stock's provisional annular profile — the reflex ring at the base's critical angle — where no independently calibrated profile exists. Off is the legacy Gaussian model and the render every existing project made; the annular road costs roughly half again as much frame time.
Halo ColourHow much the halo keeps the source's own colour instead of the film's layered red. The returning light re-enters the emulsion from below, so a colour film's ring is red whatever the light was; raising this lifts the dimmer records to the strongest record's return, and the ring brightens toward the light's colour. 0 is the film.
Return SpectrumGain over the stock's halation return spectrum. 0 preserves the stock; positive values strengthen this band. Does not create return in an absent band.
DIR CouplersMultiplier on inter-image inhibition, the mechanism behind the stock's colour separation and its Mackie lines. 0 disables it.
SeparationInterlayer inhibitor reach. 1 preserves the stock; 0 seals the layers off.
Edge ContrastWithin-layer inhibition. 1 preserves the stock's retained self-inhibition.
Fringe AmountBroad inter-layer transport fraction, 0-1. 0 keeps the stock's own transport.
Fringe Radius (µm)Broad transport Gaussian sigma on the film, 20-300 micrometers. Read only while Fringe Amount is above zero; 0 keeps the stock's own radius.
Red–Green ReachAdditional multiplier on Separation for the red–green interlayer.
Green–Blue ReachAdditional multiplier on Separation for the green–blue interlayer.
Output MediumChoose where the finished image lives. Match Film uses RA-4 paper for a still negative, the stock's native release print for a motion negative, and the direct positive for reversal film. Digital Reference is the HDR path; paper, projection, Lab Scan, Telecine and Negative are SDR. Negative is available only for negative film.
Viewing IlluminantChoose the light used to judge a physical print. Medium Reference means D50 for photo paper or calibrated 5400 K xenon for a projected release print. Only the viewing light changes; the developed print is not re-timed. Digital Reference, Lab Scan, Telecine and Negative ignore this control.
EnlargerThe lamp house a reflection print is enlarged under. Diffuser is the sheets' own diffuse read and changes nothing; Condenser prints a silver negative harder through the Callier effect. Read only where the medium is an enlarged reflection print.
Channel Contrast MatchDigital correction of channel-contrast mismatch, not physical printer timing. Off preserves the measured record slopes; raise for a more neutral crossover.
Negative ViewingHow the developed negative is read when Output Medium is Negative. Light Box sets the lamp so the clear film base sits just under white and keeps its orange. Scanner divides by the film base, so the base reads white. Other output media ignore this control.
StageWhich span of the pipeline this node performs. Full is the whole thing and is what every other setting here describes. Negative Only stops at the developed negative and writes its per-layer densities — data, not a picture — and Print Only takes exactly that back and finishes it on the selected medium, so the two in series reproduce Full. Texture Only lays the film's spatial character over the frame it is handed and leaves its colour alone. A Negative Only node must feed a Print Only node directly, with the same stock and lab settings and nothing in between: the densities are not colour and anything that grades, resamples or transforms them is not editing a picture.
Texture StagesWhether Texture Only carries this stage. Ignored by every other stage, where the strength controls below select what runs.
Render ModeDefault preserves the launch-time renderer setting. Realtime and Reference override it for this node, for both preview and delivery. Disc grain uses Reference.

Reversal films use a density-dependent grain curve with cleaner highlights and stronger grain in deep shadows, leveling off near maximum density. Grain strength stays anchored at the film's reference density. Custom packs can set the curve's exponent and shoulder with grainReversalProfile; packs explicitly selecting the legacy Selwyn law retain that behavior.

Some controls depend on the film or stage you choose. For separate Negative Only and Print Only nodes, match their development settings, including Long Exposure (s).

Resolve and Final Cut fit finished prints to the timeline's colour gamut. Colours already inside it stay unchanged. This adjustment does not apply to density data or Texture Only output.

Print exposure and viewing light

Release printing combines non-negative red, green and blue beams from a 3200 K tungsten source before the negative. Timing preserves the print film's published relative layer sensitivities and places the reference negative at the manufacturer's gross Status A aims: 1.09/1.06/1.03 for Kodak 2383 and 2393, and 1.10/1.05/1.05 for ETERNA-CP. These are processed-print measurements, not three equal densities above clear film. The result is not forced to exactly neutral display RGB. Reflection paper is set up for a visual mid-grey under D50; its colour adjustment shifts exposure, rather than adding density after development.

Viewing Illuminant changes only the light used to view the same developed print, with chromatic adaptation to the display white. Even a neutral print can change colour under another lamp. The temperature control uses Planckian spectra below 4000 K and daylight spectra from 4000 K; this is an explicit change of lamp family, not a continuous model of one lamp. Positive temperatures are bounded to 1000–25000 K and use 100 K buckets, with named standards preserved. Invalid or non-positive values use Medium Reference. An explicit 5400 K selects daylight, not the filtered-xenon Medium Reference.

The printer filters and filtered-xenon viewing spectrum are approximations; the complete calibration spectra are not published. Output remains relative to clear medium without an invented print-base spectrum. ETERNA-CP's published Gray and dye traces have an unresolved inconsistency. Channel Contrast Match is a separate digital correction, off by default; previously saved non-zero values remain active. New renders can differ from earlier versions because these corrections change physical print timing.

Film border and fringe effects

Adjacency changes development close to an image edge: the denser side gains a narrow border and the less-developed side loses density in a fringe. The command-line tool offers --adjacency-model screened-diffusion, an experimental density-dependent response with a two-Gaussian approximation to lateral diffusion with removal. It runs on the CPU and Metal renderers. Choose --adjacency-model gaussian for the original response; existing packs keep that default.

For an A/B, render the same source twice with the two model names and identical settings. Use --grain 0 --halation 0 to isolate the small edge changes. --couplers 0 disables adjacency together with inter-layer inhibition. The new model reuses the stock's radius and strength as starting values; it does not establish a new measured calibration for that stock. It models development edges, not optical halation or light leaks at the frame border.

Library clients can set FotufilmEngine.Options.adjacencyModel = .screenedDiffusion. A pack can author "adjacencyModel": "screened-diffusion". adjacencyRadiusMM retains its reference Gaussian-sigma units, with diffusion length equal to that value divided by √2. The new response acts on net formed density before reversal complementation and grain, and is bounded by the stock's development capacity.

Chromatic fringes

Fringe Amount in Film → Emulsion, beside Edge Contrast, spreads a fraction of inter-layer inhibition over a broader neighborhood. Its range is 0–100%; 0% preserves the original transport. Expand the arrow beside Fringe Amount to reveal Fringe Radius, which sets that broad Gaussian sigma in micrometers on the film, from 20–300 µm in the editor, with a starting radius of 100 µm. The radius must exceed the film's narrow inhibitor radius to act. Start with 5–20% and 50–150 µm; these are creative exploration settings, not a measured calibration for a film stock.

The fringe color follows the film's layer coupling and development curves. Uniform color patches stay unchanged apart from numerical rounding; neutral edges can acquire a tint. The existing Separation control also changes color throughout the image, while Edge Contrast changes inhibition within a layer. Monochrome films and films without inter-layer inhibition do not offer chromatic fringes. The effect does not model lens chromatic aberration or halation.

From the command line, use --fringe-amount 0.1 --fringe-radius 100; the radius is in micrometers. Use identical settings and --fringe-amount 0 for the baseline. Library clients can set FotufilmEngine.Options.chromaticFringeAmount and chromaticFringeRadiusMM (millimeters, so 100 µm is 0.1 mm). Nil uses the stock's values. Stock packs can author the same field names; absent fields mean amount 0 and radius 0.1 mm. The SDK accepts radius 0–2 mm and amount 0–1. Disabling couplers or the adjacency texture stage also disables the fringe. Existing packs and saved edits remain off by default.

Colour grading with the plugins

The engine decodes the image reaching the node into linear Rec.2020 light with mid-grey at 0.18, exposes the film with that light, develops it through characteristic curves, DIR couplers, halation and grain, and prints or scans the negative onto the selected Output Medium. The output is display-referred, with black and white set by the paper or scan, and is encoded back into the space that arrived at the node. Negative films deliver standard dynamic range; only reversal film on Digital Reference keeps light above display white. In a Rec.709 timeline the print is fitted into the Rec.709 gamut without changing luminance.

Log or linear wide-gamut timelines (DaVinci Wide Gamut / Intermediate, ACEScct, ACEScg or linear) are preferred over Rec.709 or sRGB, which clip at diffuse white and give halation no light above white. The Light & Colour controls (Exposure in stops, white balance, highlights, shadows, saturation, vibrance) act on the scene before the emulsion responds; a stop of Exposure moves the picture along the film's curve as in camera. Grading after the node regrades a finished print. Turn off host display transforms applied after the node, such as Resolve output tone mapping or an ACES output transform, or convert the print yourself to keep the paper's curve.

In an unmanaged DaVinci YRGB project Resolve labels the image Raw and the plugin assumes DaVinci Wide Gamut / Intermediate; add Color Space Transform nodes to DWG / Intermediate before the node and from it to the output space after, or set Timeline Color Space to the space that arrives. Colour-managed and ACES projects tag the image and Auto follows the tag. Final Cut Pro supplies linear light in the library's primaries, so Timeline Color Space selects primaries only. Use a Wide Gamut HDR library for log or HDR sources and place exposure and balance corrections above Fotufilm in the Video inspector.
