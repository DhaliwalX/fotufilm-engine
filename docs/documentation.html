Fotufilm guide

Try the browser editor to develop photos and videos locally in your browser, select films and output media, adjust exposure and inspect the pipeline. Large photos use overlapping tiles that include the print blur to keep tile boundaries seamless.

Fotufilm adds a film look to photos and videos. This repository includes the engine, command-line tool, Mac app, Resolve and Final Cut plugins, and browser demo.

Build and open the app

Install Xcode 26 or newer and Halide. Run swift build to build the engine and python3 tools/test-engine.py to run the complete release test suite. Run macos/build.sh --test to build the Mac app. See build support for test logs and cache controls.

Open build/macos/Fotufilm.app. Choose File → Open…, select a photo or video, and choose a film.

The Mac darkroom

The film library sits to the left of the photograph. The Darkroom panel on the right follows four stages, with the current stage highlighted in the tabs. You can move between them at any time; each adjustment updates the preview.

Small ? help buttons appear after control and section labels. Hover to read a tooltip, or click to open the explanation. Descriptions follow the selected film and options. Short status messages remain visible in the panel.

Choose Fotufilm → Settings… (⌘,) to open the separate Mac settings window. General contains defaults for new photos and film suggestions. Output groups photo, video, and negative viewing options. Film Model groups grain, halation, and color separation defaults. Changes apply immediately, and the window can stay open while you edit. Use the window’s close button or ⌘W when finished.

  1. Film: choose a stock in the library, then set its film format, age, and halation.
  2. Expose: adjust exposure, highlights, shadows, and colour balance, then use Grade directly below. Its Shadows, Midtones, and Highlights tabs show one colour pad and level slider at a time, while retaining each band’s adjustments. The light controls act before the film responds; Grade’s lift, gamma, and gain act on the developed image. Lens filters, source interpretation, video encoding, lens correction, and supported long-exposure settings also live here.
  3. Develop: set push/pull and bleach bypass, then tune grain, mottle, and the stock’s colour-separation controls.
  4. Print: choose the output medium, enlarger, and viewing light where supported. Video cadence is also here. Save with the panel’s Export button, File → Export…, or the toolbar’s Export button.

Selective and Crop are canvas tools in the toolbar above the photograph, beside the zoom controls. They are available for still photos and open their settings in the right-hand panel. Choose a darkroom stage to return to its controls. Use ⌘1–⌘4 for the four stages, ⌘5 for Selective, and ⌘6 for Crop, or choose them from the View menu. Click and hold the photograph to compare with the original. With Normal selected, Expose and Print remain available. Film and Develop show that film simulation is off; choose a film from the library to enable their controls.

Selective light and colour adjustments are saved with the photo, remain visible after closing the tool, and are included in photo exports. Undo and Redo include selective changes. Subject masks follow crop and rotation. Show Mask is a temporary preview; it is never exported.

Film packs

The Mac film sidebar offers Load custom pack…. Film Workshop is temporarily hidden on Mac and iPad, including its menu and keyboard shortcut. The pack purchase button is hidden.

Packs loaded in the Mac app are also available in the Fotufilm plugins on the same Mac and user account. Restart Resolve or Final Cut Pro after loading or updating a pack. Install matching app and plugin versions; older plugins skip packs that require a newer release.

The app and plugins include all 40 film profiles for free, including in default source builds. No activation is needed.

Thirty-three film profiles, including Gold 200, use smooth cubic interpolation through every validated digitized point from their source characteristic curves. This preserves small variations in the traced data and removes the old analytic clamp kinks. Points match within floating-point precision; they are not a claim of exact physical calibration. Beyond the source graph's exposure range, the response is extrapolated to constant density limits.

The native Metal camera renderer caches the cubic coefficients while preserving every sampled point. See the iPad performance measurements for timings and device limits.

These sampled profiles use stock schema version 2 and require an engine, app or plugin built with sampled-curve support. Older builds reject them. Existing schema version 1 profiles remain supported.

Sampled records may reach their highest density before the final exposure. The engine preserves that peak and holds the final measured value beyond the recorded range.

Print

In the iPhone photo editor, open Print → Output → Frame and choose None, Film Border, Slide Mount, a Paper Border at 4 × 6, 5 × 7, 8 × 10, or 5 × 5 inches, Carrier Border, Emulsion Border, White Mount, Black Mount, or a Square Post, Portrait Post, or Story canvas. The frames stand on a horizontally scrolled strip of framed previews, like the film strip. Film Border follows the stock’s native format, or an explicit Film Gauge choice, with physical aperture proportions, perforations, and the stock’s own base color. Negative stocks show their developed negative on a standard light box, including the color mask; reversal and instant film stay positive. Every choice other than Film Border and Slide Mount restores the selected output medium. Documented film-edge names and identification codes follow the stock and gauge, with dark lettering on negatives and light lettering on reversal film. The typeface and edge exposure are approximations; frame numbers represent a sample position, not capture metadata. Unknown markings and production serials are omitted. Paper fronts remain unlettered. Sheet film uses the stock’s documented notch pattern where available; unknown codes are omitted. Paper Border uses the selected Ektacolor Edge, ENDURA Premier, or Crystal Archive paper as a 4 × 6, 5 × 7, 8 × 10, or square 5 × 5 inch lustre print; the crop is centred on the sheet, never stretched to it. Its base follows the paper model and Viewing Light. It is inactive for screen, scans, negatives, and cinema print film. Crops are fitted inside the physical aperture without stretching or resampling the developed image. Negative film is delivered in SDR. Frames are saved with the edit, included in Undo, Redo, and photo exports, and add to export dimensions. Frames are offered in the iPhone photo editor, not video.

Slide Mount shows a reversal transparency in a plain 2 × 2 inch card mount (a 70 mm square mount for 120), with the typical mounted aperture for the gauge. The crop is fitted inside the aperture without cropping; any rebate showing beside it is the stock’s own maximum density. It is offered for slide film in 35mm and 120 and views the transparency on the light box like Film Border. Carrier Border is the darkroom look of a negative enlarged through a filed-out carrier: the clear rebate prints as a dark line at the paper’s maximum density, inside the white margin of an 8 × 10 inch print. It needs a negative stock on Ektacolor Edge, ENDURA Premier, or Crystal Archive. White Mount and Black Mount are plain margins that follow the crop on any film and output. Square Post, Portrait Post, and Story fit the photograph, uncropped and centred, inside a display-white 1 : 1, 4 : 5, or 9 : 16 canvas with a 5% margin, ready to post from any output including the Digital Reference.

Emulsion Border adds a dark, uneven band with a softly worn outer edge, subtle brown traces, and a white paper margin. It follows the photo’s crop and keeps the chosen output medium. Reflection outputs use the selected paper’s base and Viewing Light; other outputs use a neutral white mount. This is a styled border, with no specific film gauge, notch code, or manufacturer lettering. Its texture stays consistent between previews and exports. The band’s inner edge fades softly and unevenly into the photograph, as a lifted emulsion’s picture dissolves into its dark rim rather than ending at a cut line; the reach is at most 4.5% of the short side and the photograph beyond it is unchanged.

Digital Reference

Digital Reference converts colour negatives through a shared spectral receiver. Under Screen Conversion, choose Reference Exposure for calibrated mid-grey and the film's own contrast, Graded Print for film-base blacks with a softer highlight curve, or Auto Levels (default) to place the frame's bright regions near white. Auto Levels retains separation in bright windows and skies by moving the rest of the frame darker. It measures the 99.5th percentile of regional scene brightness, after exposure, and uses one green-record scale rather than independently balancing the colour channels. All three anchor black on the film base and preserve colour differences between layers. The receiver is an idealized model, not a measured scanner or display.

The Mac and iOS editors, Android photo editor, and web editor save Screen Conversion with the edit. Resolve and Final Cut expose the same menu. The CLI flag is --paper screen --digital-reference reference-exposure|graded-print|auto-levels. Fixed modes keep exposure consistent across frames; Auto Levels adapts to each frame and can vary with framing or a changing video scene. Hosts can supply a smoothed highlight measurement for video. Auto Levels does not enable local tone: when local tone is disabled, highlight and shadow adjustments remain keyed by each pixel’s brightness. Monochrome negatives take the same three choices on their own neutral curve. A transparent positive has no print curve: Reference Exposure is the slide itself, mid-grey at 18% with the clear base above display white; Graded Print brings the base to white and Auto Levels brings the frame's brightest content to white, as a slide scanner normalises each frame. Integral instant prints and other output media ignore this choice.

Two controls sit on top of the chosen style. Paper Grade (0–5, default 2) sets the contrast of the graded curve Graded Print and Auto Levels print a negative through, like a variable-contrast paper: softer grades roll highlights off earlier, harder grades later, while mid-grey and film-base black hold. Screen Exposure (±3 stops) lightens or darkens the conversion by the stated stops at mid-grey on any style — a negative's print exposure, a slide's scanner gain. The CLI flags are --screen-grade and --screen-exposure; the Android photo editor does not carry these two yet.

Profiles with a separate minimum-density spectrum use that spectrum for the film base and mask, then add absorption from the developed image dyes. This affects negative viewing, scans and optical prints. Matching published minimum-density and neutral spectra constrains the combined absorption; individual dye spectra can still be estimates.

Colour negatives can render differently from earlier versions, including when reopening saved edits with Digital Reference selected. Black-and-white negatives keep their existing direct conversion; reversal and instant films keep their direct positive view. For comparisons under the same light, choose a fixed Source Illuminant: Stock Native follows each film's own reference light.

Scene light

Source Illuminant defaults to Stock Native for RAW photos, processed photos and video. It follows the selected film's reference light: 3200 K tungsten for 500T, for example. This is a stock-balanced emulation assumption, not a reconstruction of the original scene illumination.

In the Mac editor and plugins, choose a daylight or tungsten preset, or Custom and Source Illuminant (K) (1000–25000 K), to expose the film under a different source. A daylight source on a tungsten stock retains the lighting mismatch. Warmth and Tint refine the selected light; warmer light means lower Kelvin and positive tint adds green light. Source settings do not change the printing head or Viewing Illuminant.

Output calibration stays at the stock's reference light. Extreme tint shifts are limited to physically representable light.

RAW imports retain their complete as-shot white for decoding and camera-profile correction. Capture metadata no longer automatically selects the film-exposure illuminant. Changing Source Illuminant, Warmth or Tint does not re-demosaic the image. A Kelvin value assumes a smooth Planckian/daylight spectrum; it does not identify an LED or fluorescent lamp.

In the iPhone camera, LIGHT selects simulated source illumination independently of the fixed D65 capture white balance. New sessions use Stock Native; explicit temperature selections remain fixed when films change and carry through to saved photos and editable recipes. Previously saved explicit camera choices are retained. LIGHT is unavailable in Normal mode, which does not simulate film.

The CLI --scene-kelvin 3200 explicitly chooses tungsten light; omitting it uses Stock Native. --wb is a relative mired adjustment to that source on RAW and processed inputs alike: 6504 leaves it unchanged. --tint changes its green/magenta balance. Plugins expect an already white-balanced input. Older RAW edits that relied on automatic capture-light simulation can render differently; select the former capture temperature to reproduce that assumption. Explicitly stored camera-light overrides in saved app edits are retained as Custom.

Developers can supply an exact capture xy white or an 81-sample spectrum covering 380–780 nm at 5 nm. A supplied spectrum takes precedence over the temperature/tint controls. Relative spectra are normalized to equal luminance, including lamps with no energy at 560 nm.

Print models

The output menu names each paper or print film: Kodak Ektacolor Edge, Kodak Professional Endura Premier, Fujicolor Crystal Archive Type CA, Kodak Vision 2383, Kodak Vision Premier 2393, and Fujifilm ETERNA-CP 3513DI. Each is digitised from the manufacturer's own published datasheet: Kodak E-7020, E-4070 and H-1-2383, Fujifilm AF3-0250U2, and the 2393 and ETERNA-CP 3513DI sheets. Dye spectra, layer sensitivities and characteristic curves are read from those publications rather than modelled. The film strip offers photo prints for still gauges and projection prints plus Telecine for motion-picture gauges; the film name does not change the choices. The CLI and tests also include synthetic films and a generated colour chart.

Transparent reversal film converts its characteristic Status A densities into nonnegative dye amounts by integrating the transmitted spectrum through the densitometer bands. This preserves the profile’s density response instead of equalizing its three curves. The same conversion feeds direct viewing and positive-paper exposure. A fixed viewing balance places the reference exposure at middle gray, including lookup-table interpolation; the rest of the density response retains its color crossover. Display highlight rendering remains a separate stage. Density combinations outside the supplied dye gamut use a constrained approximation. Integral instant sheets and profiles with an explicit independent minimum-density spectrum retain their own density conventions.

Ilfochrome/Cibachrome positive papers print slide film directly onto a reflection medium. Choose Ilfochrome Classic CPS.1K (Cibachrome) for normal contrast or Ilfochrome Classic CLM.1K (Cibachrome) for medium contrast. Both appear in Output Medium and the reversal-film strip. Digital Reference remains the default for slides and the only output for integral instant sheets. Positive papers are unavailable for negative film.

The two Ilfochrome profiles approximate the visual density ranges (2.0/2.05 D) and mid-tone contrasts (1.40/1.15) published in Ilford TDS 307US, August 2003, page 1. Curve endpoints and color remain approximate: the datasheet does not provide complete characteristic curves, dye spectra or layer sensitivities, so the current receiver uses Ektacolor spectra as a provisional basis. It does not reproduce measured Ilfochrome azo-dye color. Cibachrome is an alias for the same family, not a separate historical coating.

CLI IDs are ilfochrome-cps-1k and ilfochrome-clm-1k. The aliases ilfochrome and cibachrome select CPS.1K. Positive paper is SDR, supports Viewing Illuminant, Enlarger and Simulated Printer, and preserves the slide's image polarity. Paper Border leaves a dark, smooth unexposed margin; Film Border shows the original slide while retaining the saved paper selection. Increasing Paper Exposure lightens a positive-paper print; it darkens a conventional negative-paper print. For example: swift run fotufilm input.jpg output.jpg --stock provia100f --paper cibachrome.

Release prints time a correctly exposed grey card to approximately 1.0 density above clear film (10% transmission), regardless of the camera stock. Photo paper retains its 18% grey target. This makes release-print midtones darker and gives highlights more room than the previous paper-grey timing. Kodak 2383 now develops red, green and blue along separate curves from its datasheet. Existing stock-pack paperMidDensity values still load, but the selected output medium sets the grey target.

Release printing uses an approximate UV-blocked tungsten RGB additive printer, separate from the xenon light used to view the print. Complete measured printer-filter spectra are unavailable in the source sheets, so the passbands are approximate. Fujifilm ETERNA-CP's published Gray curve does not agree with its dye sum under a non-negative additive-base model; the published dyes are retained, and their neutral spectral calibration remains uncertain.

The CLI IDs are ektacolor-edge, endura-premier, crystal-archive, vision-2383, vision-2393, and eterna-cp. These identifiers are preserved, so saved print selections continue to resolve. Existing complete colour grades still load; incomplete grades are rejected.

The Enlarger menu, offered where film is optically enlarged onto reflection paper, chooses the lamp house. Diffuser is the default and reads the negative at the diffuse density the film sheets are measured in, so it changes nothing. Condenser applies the Callier effect: collimated light loses what the image scatters, so densities read at their specular values, about 1.4 times diffuse for a silver black-and-white negative and about 1.05 times for a dye negative. Each material carries its own figure, so Bleach Bypass matters here: the silver the bleach leaves behind scatters like a black-and-white negative and reads at the silver figure, and the more of it a colour negative retains, the more the condenser moves it. The print is re-timed through the scaled mid-grey, so the head shows as harder contrast rather than a darker print, and a normally processed colour negative barely moves. Scans, the negative view, Digital Reference, cinema release prints and directly viewed reversal film have no enlarger in their path and ignore the menu. The CLI flag is --enlarger diffuser|condenser.

Simulated optical printer

The engine API and CLI offer an opt-in simulated tungsten printer for negatives or slides enlarged onto compatible reflection paper. It uses a Planckian lamp and synthetic magenta/yellow filters. These are illustrative optical controls, not a measured commercial printer profile. Existing renders retain their previous behaviour when no printer profile is selected.

Select --printer simulated-tungsten. Adjust --printer-lamp from 2800 to 3600 K (default 3200), --printer-ev from −6 to +6 stops (default 0), and --printer-m and --printer-y from 0 to 1.20 (defaults 0.40 and 0.50). One stop doubles paper exposure. Filter strengths are synthetic optical-density values; exposure is relative and has no calibrated seconds. These controls are separate from film exposure, capture white balance and the finished print's viewing light.

The lamp is normalized at 560 nm. Filters change its spectrum before it passes through the film; their light loss is retained. Calibration stays at the reference 3200 K/M 0.40/Y 0.50 setup while the controls change. The profile uses the paper's three response curves directly and bypasses Channel Contrast Match. It assumes ideal exposure reciprocity and has no calibrated lamp-voltage or printer-flare model. Existing grain, halation and enlarger-head controls remain available. Scans, Digital Reference, viewed negatives, projection prints and directly viewed reversal films ignore the engine option; the CLI rejects an unsupported output.

swift run fotufilm input.jpg output.jpg --stock portra400 --paper ektacolor-edge --printer simulated-tungsten --printer-lamp 3200 --printer-ev 0.5 --printer-m 0.40 --printer-y 0.50

In Swift, assign a PrinterProfile to FotufilmEngine.Options.printer. Setting it to nil restores the previous model. PrinterProfile.matching accepts a developed-negative reference patch and a target negative patch, with fixed, density-only or density-and-colour matching. Apply its returned printer to the whole negative. The result reports the remaining display-linear RGB error and whether the requested match was reached within the profile's constraints. References are supplied explicitly; the engine does not select a face, white shirt or gray card automatically. The Mac, iPad and browser inspectors place these controls in a Lamp group with the print output controls. On iPhone, open Print and select Lamp beside Output. Enable Simulated Printer to adjust the lamp; switching it off keeps those settings for later and restores Channel Contrast Match. Lamp settings travel with saved edits, undo, and exports. The group appears only for optical paper prints. Plugin inspectors do not yet expose this profile.

Halation models

Halation Model offers Legacy and Layered Transport. Find it under Film Model in Mac and iOS settings, in the Halation group in Resolve and Final Cut, and under Film → Halation in the browser. Legacy is the default for existing settings and projects. Layered Transport traces wavelength-dependent light through the film stack. It uses an inferred stock construction where included, otherwise an illustrative stack. These constructions are estimates, not measured film stacks. Estimated Halation Shape applies only to Legacy.

Layered Transport is experimental, uses more memory and may render more slowly. Both models use the same capture illuminant; when a photo has no capture-light metadata, both use the film’s reference light to preserve neutral balance. Donor-layer stocks currently require Legacy. Browser transport uses SIMD WebAssembly and develops the whole frame; the browser uses the film’s default output medium, and stage inspection remains available with Legacy. Explicit browser Source Illuminant choices require Legacy because transport data is calibrated for the film’s reference light; Stock Native also works with RAW photos. See the model guide for the illustrative A/B, construction fields, and backend limits.

The CLI supports --transport-backend cpu|metal. Reduced bands use smooth cubic B-spline reconstruction across camera and editor paths. Use --iterations 31 to measure 30 warm renders, including median and p95 frame times and achieved throughput; image decoding and output encoding are excluded from warm timings.

Library rendering

FotufilmEngine.processChecked, developNegativeChecked, and printPositiveChecked report malformed image buffers as recoverable errors. Each image requires three finite planes with exactly width × height samples; dimensions must fit the native renderer. The legacy CPU path continues to accept empty images. Combined Legacy CPU rendering through processChecked uses strips or tiles with the complete blur overlap, keeping estimated intermediate storage within the 192 MiB default budget. Separate development and printing calls and Layered Transport have their own memory requirements. Input and output frames, kernel compilation, and caches are additional memory. If the spatial support cannot fit, checked rendering reports an error. To explicitly budget more memory for a broad diffusion effect, call processChecked(linearRGB:cpuMemoryBudget:) with the allowed intermediate bytes. The nonthrowing process convenience retains its fatal-error behavior; use checked rendering to handle failures.

The CPU processSRGB8 and processDisplayP38 helpers accept RGB or premultiplied RGBA and return the same format. RGBA output preserves alpha, multiplies encoded RGB by coverage, and returns zero RGB for fully transparent pixels. Metal byte rendering follows the same alpha convention. Floating-point image planes contain unassociated RGB.

FilmStockPack.reload() invalidates the current pack generation. Concurrent callers share one load per generation; a load that began before a reload cannot overwrite the newer pack or its load error.

Swift package clients on macOS can reuse compiled Halide CPU and Metal kernels across launches. The cache stores compiled code only; input photos, edit settings, film lookup tables and rendered results remain outside it. Cache entries are tied to the engine binary and compilation environment. A changed binary or unavailable cache uses fresh compilation. Packaged Mac and plugin AOT builds keep their existing precompiled kernels.

Use FilmEngineInvocation(validating:options:width:height:) or the engine’s processChecked, developNegativeChecked and printPositiveChecked methods to handle unsupported push/pull settings as FilmDevelopmentError. Checked Metal preparation propagates the same error; Boolean preparation methods return false. The original nonthrowing entry points require known-valid settings and report the development error if that requirement is violated.

HalideMetalFilmRenderer.minimumPeakBytes returns an optional estimate. Unwrap it before calculating a memory budget; nil means the requested development condition cannot be prepared. canRender returns false in that case.

Scanned negatives

Choose File → Import Scanned Negative… and open a RAW or unconverted image with its clear film border visible. Drag over an unexposed border patch, choose the closest film, and use Preview Positive. Import Positive opens the result with Normal selected and the Crop tool ready.

Drag the four crop corners independently, then leave Crop or press Return to straighten the selected area. Corner edits support undo, saved edits and full-resolution export. Rotation and flipping keep the four-corner selection attached to the image. Drags use a cached display preview; film thumbnails refresh after leaving Crop. Choose an aspect ratio for a rectangular crop, or Reset Crop to start again.

Conversion is approximate and does not require a calibration wedge. Use unadjusted scans and their file colour profile; select Linear Samples only for a known linear scan. The positive retains full resolution in a 16-bit TIFF. Reimport the original to change border sampling or film choice. See the input requirements and conversion limits.

Developers can convert linear negative scans with the scanned-negative library API. Supply dark and light references plus a calibration mapping for the capture setup and film. The API recovers film densities and enters the existing print stage, preserving halation and grain already in the film. Measured scanner profiles and CLI controls are not included.

Browser editor

The CPU and WebGPU film renderers preserve the same scene-linear float32 response for identical pixels, settings and film packs. Display conversion and 8-bit sRGB export happen after film processing.

The browser editor is experimental, as marked in its toolbar. It uses the Mac app’s film sidebar, photo canvas, and Darkroom heading with compact Film, Expose, Develop, and Print tabs. Halation return

Halation Return sets the red returned/direct exposure percentage while preserving the film’s relative red, green and blue returns. CineStill 800T and 400D start at their measured 3% — raise it toward 10–15% for the heavier glow familiar from web scans; other films retain their stock values. The editor saves an override with the edit. Use Film Return clears the override and follows the selected film again. Existing edits without an override follow the current stock default.

The Mac and browser editors offer this percentage under Film → Halation. The CLI accepts --halation-return 12, with values from 0 through 100; 0 disables return. It applies before the existing Halation amount, Halo Colour and Return Spectrum controls. The same percentage controls both Legacy and Layered Transport. This is an adjustable rendering default; film development and the output medium determine how visible the halo becomes.

Authoring crystal populations

Every film pack must provide crystalGrainPopulation for Organic Crystals. radiusSpan bounds the largest-to-smallest radius ratio on the sensitivity-class ladder (1–20). sublayerShares supplies four positive density-weight fractions, fastest to slowest, summing to 1. These set cumulative boundaries for whole speed classes; the sublayer weights are then refitted to the film's tone curve. They are not exact final crystal-count or silver-mass fractions. Different allocations can change the accuracy of that curve fit and should be checked before adopting a profile.

"crystalGrainPopulation": {
  "radiusSpan": 3,
  "sublayerShares": [0.20, 0.25, 0.25, 0.30],
  "coatingDensityScale": 1
}

This example is an experiment, not a measured film-family calibration. Width and allocation retain the film's RMS granularity anchor. coatingDensityScale (0.25–4) then multiplies the effective crystal count and divides each crystal's dye contribution by the same factor, preserving mean demand and pool capacity. A value of 2 predicts RMS grain lower by √2. It deliberately changes the RMS anchor; keep 1 when matching a measured sheet value. It does not simulate an increase in physical silver coating mass, sensitization, turbidity or sharpness loss.

The population object and all three fields are required. Missing or incomplete settings are rejected. Bundled profiles explicitly author radius span 6, shares [0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25], and density scale 1. Standard and Particle do not use these parameters. A push or pull uses the reference stock's population settings. To compare a candidate, render the same source with --grain-model crystals, the same seed, format and output medium, and inspect both tone reproduction and grain at full resolution. Bundled profiles retain their current texture settings.

Print exposure and viewing light

Release printing combines non-negative red, green and blue beams from a 3200 K tungsten source before the negative. Timing preserves the print film's published relative layer sensitivities and places the reference negative at the manufacturer's gross Status A aims: 1.09/1.06/1.03 for Kodak 2383 and 2393, and 1.10/1.05/1.05 for ETERNA-CP. These are processed-print measurements, not three equal densities above clear film. The result is not forced to exactly neutral display RGB. Reflection paper is set up for a visual mid-grey under D50; its colour adjustment shifts exposure, rather than adding density after development.

Viewing Illuminant changes only the light used to view the same developed print, with chromatic adaptation to the display white. Even a neutral print can change colour under another lamp. The temperature control uses Planckian spectra below 4000 K and daylight spectra from 4000 K; this is an explicit change of lamp family, not a continuous model of one lamp. Positive temperatures are bounded to 1000–25000 K and use 100 K buckets, with named standards preserved. Invalid or non-positive values use Medium Reference. An explicit 5400 K selects daylight, not the filtered-xenon Medium Reference.

The printer filters and filtered-xenon viewing spectrum are approximations; the complete calibration spectra are not published. Output remains relative to clear medium without an invented print-base spectrum. ETERNA-CP's published Gray and dye traces have an unresolved inconsistency. Channel Contrast Match is a separate digital correction, off by default; previously saved non-zero values remain active. New renders can differ from earlier versions because these corrections change physical print timing.

Film border and fringe effects

Adjacency changes development close to an image edge: the denser side gains a narrow border and the less-developed side loses density in a fringe. The command-line tool offers --adjacency-model screened-diffusion, an experimental density-dependent response with a two-Gaussian approximation to lateral diffusion with removal. It runs on the CPU and Metal renderers. Choose --adjacency-model gaussian for the original response; existing packs keep that default.

For an A/B, render the same source twice with the two model names and identical settings. Use --grain 0 --halation 0 to isolate the small edge changes. --couplers 0 disables adjacency together with inter-layer inhibition. The new model reuses the stock's radius and strength as starting values; it does not establish a new measured calibration for that stock. It models development edges, not optical halation or light leaks at the frame border.

Library clients can set FotufilmEngine.Options.adjacencyModel = .screenedDiffusion. A pack can author "adjacencyModel": "screened-diffusion". adjacencyRadiusMM retains its reference Gaussian-sigma units, with diffusion length equal to that value divided by √2. The new response acts on net formed density before reversal complementation and grain, and is bounded by the stock's development capacity.

Chromatic fringes

Fringe Amount in Film → Emulsion, beside Edge Contrast, spreads a fraction of inter-layer inhibition over a broader neighborhood. Its range is 0–100%; 0% preserves the original transport. Expand the arrow beside Fringe Amount to reveal Fringe Radius, which sets that broad Gaussian sigma in micrometers on the film, from 20–300 µm in the editor, with a starting radius of 100 µm. The radius must exceed the film's narrow inhibitor radius to act. Start with 5–20% and 50–150 µm; these are creative exploration settings, not a measured calibration for a film stock.

The fringe color follows the film's layer coupling and development curves. Uniform color patches stay unchanged apart from numerical rounding; neutral edges can acquire a tint. The existing Separation control also changes color throughout the image, while Edge Contrast changes inhibition within a layer. Monochrome films and films without inter-layer inhibition do not offer chromatic fringes. The effect does not model lens chromatic aberration or halation.

From the command line, use --fringe-amount 0.1 --fringe-radius 100; the radius is in micrometers. Use identical settings and --fringe-amount 0 for the baseline. Library clients can set FotufilmEngine.Options.chromaticFringeAmount and chromaticFringeRadiusMM (millimeters, so 100 µm is 0.1 mm). Nil uses the stock's values. Stock packs can author the same field names; absent fields mean amount 0 and radius 0.1 mm. The SDK accepts radius 0–2 mm and amount 0–1. Disabling couplers or the adjacency texture stage also disables the fringe. Existing packs and saved edits remain off by default.

Colour grading with the plugins

The engine decodes the image reaching the node into linear Rec.2020 light with mid-grey at 0.18, exposes the film with that light, develops it through characteristic curves, DIR couplers, halation and grain, and prints or scans the negative onto the selected Output Medium. The output is display-referred, with black and white set by the paper or scan, and is encoded back into the space that arrived at the node in Timeline mode, or into the independently selected Output Color Space in Resolve’s Fotufilm mode. Negative films deliver standard dynamic range; only reversal film on Digital Reference keeps light above display white. In a Rec.709 timeline the print is fitted into the Rec.709 gamut without changing luminance.

Log or linear wide-gamut timelines (DaVinci Wide Gamut / Intermediate, ACEScct, ACEScg or linear) are preferred over Rec.709 or sRGB, which clip at diffuse white and give halation no light above white. The Light & Colour controls (Exposure in stops, white balance, highlights, shadows, saturation, vibrance) act on the scene before the emulsion responds; a stop of Exposure moves the picture along the film's curve as in camera. Grading after the node regrades a finished print. Turn off host display transforms applied after the node, such as Resolve output tone mapping or an ACES output transform, or convert the print yourself to keep the paper's curve.

Resolve Color Management offers two modes. Timeline preserves the existing workflow: select Timeline Color Space (or Auto) and Fotufilm returns the same encoding. Fotufilm exposes separate Input Color Space and Output Color Space menus. Camera-log input is decoded directly to scene-linear Rec.2020, developed once, and encoded into the chosen output without an additional tone map. The choices persist per node and do not animate. See the supported input and output spaces.

For Apple Log in an unmanaged DaVinci YRGB project, choose Fotufilm mode, Apple Log / Rec.2020 input, and Rec.709 / Gamma 2.4 output; surrounding CST nodes are unnecessary. Match Resolve’s monitoring and export settings to the output. Input always means the pixels reaching the node, including any upstream host conversion. The plugin does not change project settings or export tags. Negative Only ignores the output selection, Print Only ignores the input selection, and Texture Only converts scene light between the selected spaces.

Realtime and Reference support the same colour-space choices, including direct Rec.709 and sRGB output at portrait and landscape resolutions. The colour transforms do not require Reference mode. For bands or GPU failures with an older plugin, see build support.

In Timeline mode with Auto selected, an unmanaged DaVinci YRGB project labels the image Raw and Fotufilm assumes DaVinci Wide Gamut / Intermediate; add Color Space Transform nodes to DWG / Intermediate before the node and from it to the output space after, or set Timeline Color Space to the space that arrives. Colour-managed and ACES projects tag the image and Auto follows the tag. Final Cut Pro supplies linear light in the library's primaries, so Timeline Color Space selects primaries only. Use a Wide Gamut HDR library for log or HDR sources and place exposure and balance corrections above Fotufilm in the Video inspector.
