Overview & quick build

This repository contains the Fotufilm rendering engine, command-line tool (fotufilm), cross-platform Flutter editor (macOS, Linux, Android, and WebGPU browser demo), and native video plug-ins for DaVinci Resolve and Final Cut Pro. Every build includes all 47 calibrated film profiles under CC BY-SA 4.0 alongside Apache-2.0 engine code, with no activation or license keys.

To build and test locally on macOS, install Xcode 26 or newer and Halide:

swift build
python3 tools/test-engine.py
./flutter/build.sh

Open build/flutter-host/Fotufilm.app, choose File → Open…, select a photo or video, and pick a film stock. See Build & web support for Linux, WebAssembly/WebGPU, sanitizer codec checks, and kernel cache options.

Desktop & browser darkroom

The desktop app and the browser editor share one workspace: the Films and Presets library on the left, the live canvas and open-photo strip in the center, and the four-stage Darkroom inspector on the right. Drag the divider between the canvas and either sidebar to resize panels (double-click or press Return on a focused divider to reset; the canvas always retains at least 420 px).

Above the Darkroom heading, a live chart displays the edit’s RGB tone curve from 0 to 255 through the film, output medium, and grade, overlaid on a histogram of the developed picture. Small ? buttons beside labels reveal context-aware explanations for the active film and medium.

Four darkroom stages

  1. Film (⌘1): Select a stock in the library, then set its physical Film Format, Expired age, and Halation controls.
  2. Expose (⌘2): Adjust scene Exposure, Highlights, Shadows, Camera Preflash, Source Illuminant, Temperature, Tint, Saturation, Vibrance, optical Filters, and Lens Correction—all acting in scene-linear light before the emulsion responds. Below them, the three-way Grade (Shadows, Midtones, Highlights) and Black Point / White Point sliders shape the finished print without clipping.
  3. Develop (⌘3): Set measured Push / Pull development and Bleach Bypass, choose between Standard and crystal-level Film grain models, and adjust interlayer Couplers, Separation, Edge Contrast, and chromatic fringes.
  4. Print (⌘4): Choose the Output Medium (defaulting to Match Film for new edits), Viewing Illuminant, Paper Surface, optical Enlarger / Simulated Printer lamp settings, and Frame borders, then export.

Canvas tools, library & batch export

  • Selective (⌘5) & Crop (⌘6 / C): Sample a point to isolate similar Color or Light (plus automatic Subject masks on macOS), or crop with locked aspect ratios, straighten, perspective tilt, and independent four-corner handles. Adjustments travel with undo, saved edits, and full-resolution exports.
  • Auto Adjust (⌘⇧A / Ctrl+Shift+A): Computes Exposure, Highlights, and Shadows from regional scene brightness and the active film’s latitude; switching films while Auto is active recalculates automatically.
  • Library (L) & Strip: Add folders on disk to browse JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, HEIC, TIFF, EXR, camera RAW, and video files with instant embedded-preview thumbnails, 1–5 star ratings, filename search, and automatic edit persistence. On desktop, opening a single file also loads sibling media from its folder into the bottom strip.
  • Floating Histogram (H): Switch between Log or Linear counts and RGB, Luma, Chroma, OKLab L, or OKLab a/b views.
  • Tiled previews & Export All: Large photos and zoomed previews render in overlapping tiles that include full grain and halation neighborhoods so tile seams never appear. When multiple photos are open on desktop, Export All develops and encodes them into a target folder concurrently while pipelining decode, GPU/CPU development, and file encoding.
  • Settings (⌘,): Opens a dedicated window on macOS with General, Output (including Full vs. Fast video quality), and Film Model defaults.

Mobile & compact editor

On phones and narrow viewports, the darkroom places the photograph between a clean top bar (Add, Undo, Redo, More, Export) and a bottom row of editing groups: Film Strip, Grain, Lens, Print, Light & Color, Select, and Crop (or Film Strip, Negative, Print, Light & Color, and Crop for scanned negatives).

Tapping any group opens a resizable sheet with tabbed subpages. Sliders track relative finger movement from wherever you touch and pause subtly at default values. Pinch-to-zoom refines only the visible viewport at full device-pixel resolution once movement settles for 300 ms, while preserving full-frame grain coordinates and whole-photo tone metering.

On iPhone, video export defaults to Faster Export with Maximum Detail selectable in export options, and camera stills develop at approximately 12 MP with Legacy halation.

Scene light, RAW & wide-gamut media

Source Illuminant & white balance

Source Illuminant defaults to Stock Native for RAW photos, processed photos, and video, exposing each stock under its calibrated reference lamp (such as 3200 K tungsten for Vision3 500T or 5500 K daylight for Portra 400). Choose a daylight/tungsten preset or Custom (1000–25000 K) to simulate shooting under mismatched lighting. Temperature (relative mired shift; 6504 is neutral) and Tint refine the scene light without altering the enlarger lamp or Viewing Illuminant.

In the CLI, pass --scene-kelvin 3200 to select tungsten explicitly, along with --wb and --tint. Swift callers can also pass an exact capture CIE xy white or an 81-sample spectrum (380–780 nm at 5 nm).

Camera RAW, HDR JPEG, TIFF, PNG, EXR & Video

  • Camera RAW: Apple hosts decode RAW via Core Image; Linux, Android, and Web hosts decode via LibRaw 0.22.2 in linear Rec.2020 with highlight recovery, DNG baseline exposure, and bundled measured camera spectral profiles where available. See the portable codec guide for bounded memory budgets.
  • HDR Gain-Map JPEG: ISO and Adobe gain-map JPEGs open in full scene-linear range. Under Expose → Source Interpretation, choose Automatic / Full Range to retain highlight headroom above diffuse white or Standard Range for the SDR base image.
  • 16-bit PNG, Bounded TIFF & Linear EXR: Dedicated decoders preserve 16-bit PNGs, 1/2/4/8/16-bit integer and 32-bit float TIFFs (with embedded ICC profiles via Little CMS), and single-part half/float32 OpenEXR scenes up to 120 MP without 8-bit canvas clamping.
  • Still & DPX Export: Export 8-bit PNG, JPEG, WebP, HEIC (Apple), lossless 16-bit Display P3 TIFF, or—when Output Medium is set to Cineon Log—10-bit Cineon DPX (0.002 D per code value, mid-gray at 445) for downstream grading.
  • Video & Camera Log: Import MP4, MOV, M4V, WebM, or MKV clips, set trim points, and decode Standard SDR/HDR or 10 camera-log formats (Apple Log, Apple Log 2, S-Log3, S-Log2, F-Log, F-Log2, F-Log2 C, V-Log, HLG) directly into scene-linear Rec.2020 on Metal or WebGPU.

Print models, Digital Reference & enlargers

Physical papers & cinema release prints

Output Medium includes nine digitized photographic papers and motion-picture release print stocks—Kodak Ektacolor Edge (ektacolor-edge), Kodak Professional Endura Premier (endura-premier), Fujicolor Crystal Archive Type CA (crystal-archive), Kodak Vision 2383 (vision-2383), Kodak Vision Premier 2393 (vision-2393), Fujifilm ETERNA-CP 3513DI (eterna-cp), Fujifilm ETERNA-CP 3521XD (eterna-cp-3521xd), Kodak EXR 5386 (exr-5386), and Agfa CP30 (agfa-cp30)—plus two positive reversal papers, Ilfochrome Classic CPS.1K (ilfochrome-cps-1k / cibachrome) and CLM.1K (ilfochrome-clm-1k). See print-stocks.md for modeling details.

Cinema release prints time mid-gray to each print stock’s gross Status A LAD aim (~1.0 density above clear film) and expose Red Light, Green Light, and Blue Light printer points (1–50, aim 25-25-25, 0.025 log E per point; CLI --printer-red, --printer-green, --printer-blue). Viewing Illuminant changes only the viewing lamp with chromatic adaptation to display white.

Digital Reference & Lab Scan

  • Digital Reference (--paper screen): Converts negatives and slides through an idealized spectral receiver with three Screen Conversion modes—Auto Levels (default lab-style per-frame timing), Graded Print, and fixed Reference Exposure—plus Paper Grade (0–5, --screen-grade), Scan Exposure (--screen-exposure), and Cyan/Red, Magenta/Green, Yellow/Blue trim (--screen-cyan, --screen-magenta, --screen-yellow).
  • Lab Scan: Emulates a minilab scanner’s per-frame setup, automatic channel leveling, regional highlight/shadow dodging, and signature color finishing (adjustable via Dodging and Scanner Look).

Enlarger heads & simulated tungsten printer

On enlarged reflection prints, Enlarger (--enlarger diffuser|condenser) switches between diffuse density reading (Diffuser) and collimated Callier-effect contrast (Condenser, strongest on silver B&W negatives and bleach-bypassed color negatives).

Enabling Simulated Printer (CLI --printer simulated-tungsten) activates an illustrative Planckian tungsten lamp (--printer-lamp 2800–3600 K), relative paper exposure (--printer-ev ±6 EV), and synthetic optical-density filtration (--printer-m, --printer-y):

swift run fotufilm input.jpg output.jpg --stock portra400 --paper ektacolor-edge \
  --printer simulated-tungsten --printer-lamp 3200 --printer-ev 0.5 --printer-m 0.40 --printer-y 0.50

Scanned negatives & roll calibration

Import Scanned Negative… treats an unadjusted camera RAW, TIFF, PNG, or JPEG scan of developed film as a negative halfway through the pipeline, feeding recovered densities directly into the print stage while preserving the film’s real grain and halation. Choose a matching negative stock to print through physical papers, or select Normal for direct base-inverted positive editing.

  • Film Base & Light Frame: Sample unexposed clear film with Pick Clear Film, and optionally supply a bare-light reference frame on desktop to flatten backlight falloff.
  • Scan Setup & Correction: Specify Scanned With (Auto camera profile or Film Scanner), Scanned Under (White LED Panel, Phone or Tablet Screen, Halogen Lamp, or merged Red, Green and Blue LEDs), Colour Separation (0–1 dye-crosstalk inversion), and Measure Flare (0–2% stray light).
  • Roll Balance: In a folder of negative scans, open the Roll tab and click Measure Roll to share highlight color balance and inter-channel contrast across every frame on the roll. See scanned-negatives.md and automatic-negative-conversion.md for input requirements and library APIs.

Film packs, sampled curves, halation & grain

Thirty-three bundled profiles use schema version 2 smooth cubic interpolation across every validated point of their digitized characteristic curves (cached for real-time Metal rendering; see performance-sampled-curves.md). Custom packs loaded in the Mac app are shared automatically with the Resolve and Final Cut plug-ins on the same user account after restarting the NLE.

Halation models & return

Halation Model offers Legacy (default) and experimental Layered Transport, which traces wavelength-dependent scatter through multi-layer film stacks (see layered-transport.md; CLI --transport-backend cpu|metal). Halation Return (--halation-return 0–100) sets red returned/direct exposure percentage: CineStill 800T and 400D start at their measured 3% and can be raised toward 10–15% for heavier scan glow.

Adjacency & chromatic fringes

The CLI supports --adjacency-model gaussian|screened-diffusion (or FotufilmEngine.Options.adjacencyModel in Swift) for edge development effects, and Fringe Amount / Fringe Radius (--fringe-amount, --fringe-radius) spread interlayer inhibition across 20–300 μm neighborhoods around color boundaries.

Authoring crystal populations

Every film pack provides a required crystalGrainPopulation object for the physical Film grain model (--grain-model film; see film-grain.md):

"crystalGrainPopulation": {
  "radiusSpan": 3,
  "sublayerShares": [0.20, 0.25, 0.25, 0.30],
  "coatingDensityScale": 1
}

radiusSpan (1–20) sets the largest-to-smallest crystal radius ratio across sensitivity classes, sublayerShares supplies four positive fractions summing to 1, and coatingDensityScale (0.25–4, keep 1 to preserve measured RMS granularity) scales effective crystal count inversely against per-crystal dye yield. Bundled profiles use radiusSpan: 6, equal 0.25 shares, and coatingDensityScale: 1.

Video plug-ins & Resolve controls

Both DaVinci Resolve (OpenFX) and Final Cut Pro / Motion (FxPlug) group parameters in optical order: Input, Film, Light & Colour, Lens & Filters, Development, Grain, Halation, Colour Separation, Output, and Pipeline. Read-only status rows (Resolved Format, Resolved Medium, Effective Renderer) confirm active pipeline state.

Prefer wide-gamut log or linear timelines (DaVinci Wide Gamut / Intermediate, ACEScct, ACEScg, or Final Cut Wide Gamut HDR) over Rec.709/sRGB so specular highlights above diffuse white reach the halation and film shoulder stages intact. In Resolve, choose Color Management: Fotufilm to decode camera log (such as Apple Log) and encode display output (such as Rec.709 / Gamma 2.4) in a single node without external CSTs, or keep Timeline mode to return the incoming timeline encoding. See the Resolve guide and Final Cut guide.

Timeline Color SpaceWhat this node is being handed — the one control that is not taste. The film model is scene-referred in linear Rec.2020: mid-grey at 0.18, specular highlights above 1.0. The input is decoded to that and the result encoded back, so a wrong setting shows the emulsion the wrong light and lands the characteristic curves whole stops off. Auto reads the space the host tags the clip with, where the host says. A display-referred space (Rec.709, sRGB) clips at diffuse white, leaving halation nothing bright to scatter; a wide-gamut log or linear timeline keeps the highlights the model was built for.
Input Color SpaceThe actual input pixels, after any upstream nodes or host color management. In an unmanaged DaVinci YRGB project, camera log can enter directly without an input CST. Only used in Fotufilm mode; Print Only receives density data and ignores this setting.
Output Color SpaceEncode the finished print into this space without another tone map. Match Resolve's monitoring and export settings to it. Only used in Fotufilm mode; Negative Only writes density data and ignores this setting. Texture Only returns scene light in this space.
StockThe emulsion. Each is a measured stock: its own spectral sensitivity, characteristic curves, couplers, halation and granularity.
Film FormatThe gauge the frame is exposed on. The image height maps onto the gauge's frame height, so a smaller format is enlarged more and shows coarser grain, wider halation and stronger adjacency — the same emulsion at a different magnification. Match Film takes the gauge the chosen stock is known on.
Film Frame Coverage (%)Short edge of the film frame retained after cropping. 100 uses the full frame; 50 enlarges half the frame. Changes grain, halation and other spatial scales, without cropping pixels. Account for any upstream resize yourself.
ExposureCamera exposure, in stops.
Temperature (K)Spectral scene temperature. Lower Kelvin adds warm light. Relative in mired to Source Illuminant; 6504 leaves the selected or stock-native lamp unchanged.
TintGreen/magenta balance of the illuminant.
HighlightsScene-referred highlight recovery, applied before the film model. Keyed to each pixel's regional brightness, so pulling a sky down moves the sky as one piece.
ShadowsThe same shift, fading in below mid-grey.
Regional Tone MaskOff, the highlight and shadow shifts key to each pixel's own luminance instead of to the region it sits in. Identical output when both rest at zero.
Camera PreflashPre-exposure of the taking film in linear scene radiance units, lifting shadows without shifting highlights.
SaturationChroma multiplier applied to the scene before the film responds. 1 leaves it untouched.
VibranceChroma boost weighted toward the least colourful pixels; already-vivid colours are left alone.
Source IlluminantLight presented to the film; Stock Native follows the selected film's reference. Temperature and Tint adjust this light. Custom sources use a daylight or Planckian spectrum, not a measured LED spectrum.
Source Illuminant (K)Custom source light before Temperature and Tint; used only when Source Illuminant is Custom.
Filter 1An absorbing filter on the front of the lens. It is integrated spectrally against the chosen film's own three layer sensitivities, which is why the same filter is a different filter on a different stock — an 85B is a correction on tungsten film and a heavy warm cast on daylight film. It also adds the veiling glare of two more air-glass faces, whatever the Veiling Glare slider says — and that added glare is why this control is live only on the Full stage: no kernel this build carries can measure it in a span that ends at the developed negative.
Filter 2A second filter, behind the first. They stack in the order given: their transmittances multiply, and the gap between them makes a ghost of its own.
Filter 3A third filter, behind the second.
MeteringHow the exposure was set with those filters fitted. Metered through is the camera's own photopic cell reading the light that got past the glass, and is the default; it can underexpose behind a narrow filter, because the meter does not use the film's sensitivity. Filter factor is the published compensation, worked out against the emulsion, which restores the luminance record without cancelling the colour change. None is a fixed manual exposure, so the light the filter took lands on the film as underexposure. Ignored with no filter fitted, and live only on the Full stage, which is the only span a filter is live in.
DiffusionA diffusion filter on the front of the lens. A share of the light meets a particle and leaves in a new direction, and the lens images it somewhere else on the frame; the share that missed every particle is untouched, which is why a diffused picture keeps its edges instead of going soft. The black families carry absorbing particles: the blacks still lift and the highlights bloom much less.
Diffusion GradeThe particle loading a product line's 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1 and 2 name: one formulation more heavily loaded, so the grade moves how much light takes part and never how far it goes. Ignored with no diffusion filter fitted.
Focal LengthThe taking lens's focal length in millimetres, read only by the diffusion filter: a ray deviated by an angle ahead of the lens lands focal length times that angle off its unscattered position, so the same filter glows bigger on a longer lens, exactly as it does in the world. 0 — the default — is the gauge's own normal lens, which is what the grade numbering on a filter's ring is calibrated around.
Veiling GlareVeiling glare from the taking lens, as a multiplier on the stock's figure. It defaults to 0 because a photographed clip already carries the glare of the lens that shot it, and this stage would veil the shadows a second time. Raise it for light that has met no glass — a render or a synthetic chart — or to stand in for glass worse than the camera's.
Filter CoatingCoating on every fitted absorbing and diffusion filter. Changes transmission and added glare. Multicoated preserves the existing filter look.
Push / PullPush or pull conditions for this film's stated developer and process: measured from its own published curves, carried from a sister film's published curves, or estimated from the process's published push response. The control is disabled when the stock pack lists no conditions.
Bleach BypassHow much of the developed silver the bleach leaves in the negative. The retained silver is a black-and-white image over the colour one: contrast up, chroma down, together.
Film Age (years)Years the roll sat past its process-by date. Speed falls a stop a decade with the blue-sensitive layer going first, base fog rises, and grain rises with the fog — the muddy, crossed toe of an old roll.
Long Exposure (s)Exposure duration for the stock's measured reciprocity response. 0 disables the override. Corrections stop at the last measured duration; no motion blur is added. Use the same value in paired Negative Only and Print Only nodes.
GrainMultiplier on the stock's measured granularity. 0 disables it.
MottleUse the stock's coarse grain mixture, or set a custom share. Full mode only.
Mottle Amount (%)Share of grain variance carried by coarse clumping. Total calibrated granularity is preserved. Custom mottle uses the engine's video delivery size.
Grain AnimationTimeline changes grain each frame. Frozen uses the same field at every time; the texture still responds to changes in the image.
Grain ModelStandard uses the calibrated clump field. Film lays the stock's crystals at fixed places on the emulsion and averages the light through each pixel's patch of film.
Grain SizeFilm grain model only. Magnifies the film's crystals and clouds, with their fluctuation scaled down so the 48 µm aperture still reads the sheet's granularity: coarser, softer grain above 1, finer below.
Colour GrainFilm grain model only. 100% keeps the three layers' grain independent, as the emulsion lays it; 0% gives them one shared grain. The grain's total strength is kept.
Red LayerFilm grain model only. Multiplier on the red-sensitive layer's grain, 1 at the sheet's granularity.
Green LayerFilm grain model only. Multiplier on the green-sensitive layer's grain, 1 at the sheet's granularity.
Blue LayerFilm grain model only. Multiplier on the blue-sensitive layer's grain, 1 at the sheet's granularity.
Scan SoftnessFilm grain model only. The side of film each pixel averages, in pixels: 1 is a scan as sharp as its pitch, wider softens the grain, narrower sharpens it.
Grain SeedSame seed and same frame give the same grain. Grain also advances with the timeline, so a still frame is still, and a moving one is not.
Halation ModelLegacy or layered optical transport. Layered uses an inferred stock construction where included, otherwise an illustrative stack.
HalationMultiplier on the fraction of light the base returns. On the legacy model it scales the light going down rather than the finished halo, so raising it widens the halo as well as brightening it — the way a thinner antihalation layer would. With Estimated Halation Shape on, the film's geometry is pinned and this scales the amount alone. 1 is the stock's authored look — the sheet's look scale times the measured film, whose calibrated returns sit at the patent-floor absorber densities and are all but invisible on their own. The measured film survives at 1 over the look scale (0.025 on a rem-jet stock). Typing past the slider reaches 100.
Estimated Halation ShapeRenders halation through the stock's provisional annular profile — the reflex ring at the base's critical angle — where no independently calibrated profile exists. Off is the legacy Gaussian model and the render every existing project made; the annular road costs roughly half again as much frame time.
Halo ColourHow much the halo keeps the source's own colour instead of the film's layered red. The returning light re-enters the emulsion from below, so a colour film's ring is red whatever the light was; raising this lifts the dimmer records to the strongest record's return, and the ring brightens toward the light's colour. 0 is the film.
Return SpectrumGain over the stock's halation return spectrum. 0 preserves the stock; positive values strengthen this band. Does not create return in an absent band.
DIR CouplersMultiplier on inter-image inhibition, the mechanism behind the stock's colour separation and its Mackie lines. 0 disables it.
SeparationInterlayer inhibitor reach. 1 preserves the stock; 0 seals the layers off.
Edge ContrastWithin-layer inhibition. 1 preserves the stock's retained self-inhibition.
Fringe AmountBroad inter-layer transport fraction, 0-1. 0 keeps the stock's own transport.
Fringe Radius (µm)Broad transport Gaussian sigma on the film, 20-300 micrometers. Read only while Fringe Amount is above zero; 0 keeps the stock's own radius.
Red–Green ReachAdditional multiplier on Separation for the red–green interlayer.
Green–Blue ReachAdditional multiplier on Separation for the green–blue interlayer.
Output MediumChoose where the finished image lives. Match Film uses RA-4 paper for a still negative, the stock's native release print for a motion negative, and the direct positive for reversal film. Digital Reference is the HDR path; paper, projection, Lab Scan, Telecine and Negative are SDR. Cineon Log is a flat scan of the negative for grading, delivered as Cineon code values rather than a picture. Slide film also supports Ilfochrome/Cibachrome positive paper. Negative and Cineon Log are available only for negative film.
Viewing IlluminantChoose the light used to judge a physical print. Medium Reference means D50 for photo paper or calibrated 5400 K xenon for a projected release print. Only the viewing light changes; the developed print is not re-timed. Digital Reference, Lab Scan, Telecine, Cineon Log and Negative ignore this control.
EnlargerThe lamp house a reflection print is enlarged under. Diffuser is the sheets' own diffuse read and changes nothing; Condenser prints a silver negative harder through the Callier effect. Read only where the medium is an enlarged reflection print.
Screen ConversionReference Exposure holds calibrated exposure; Graded Print softens highlights; Auto Levels meters frame highlights. A positive is normalised to SDR instead: its base or its brightest content to white. Digital Reference only.
Paper GradeVariable-contrast paper grade 0–5 for Graded Print and Auto Levels on Digital Reference: grade 2 is the calibrated curve, softer grades lower contrast in the highlights and shadows, harder grades raise it. Mid-grey and film-base black hold. Negatives only.
Scan ExposureExposure of the Digital Reference conversion in stops on top of the chosen style, or of a Lab Scan at mid-grey; positive lightens. A negative's print exposure, a slide's scanner gain, a lab scanner's density key.
Cyan / RedDigital Reference and Lab Scan colour negatives: positive adds cyan, negative adds red. Manual correction after Auto Levels; relative units, not scanner steps.
Channel Contrast MatchDigital correction of channel-contrast mismatch, not physical printer timing. Off preserves the measured record slopes; raise for a more neutral crossover.
Magenta / GreenDigital Reference and Lab Scan colour negatives: positive adds magenta, negative adds green. Manual correction after Auto Levels; relative units, not scanner steps.
Yellow / BlueDigital Reference and Lab Scan colour negatives: positive adds yellow, negative adds blue. Manual correction after Auto Levels; relative units, not scanner steps.
DodgingLab Scan negatives: the scanner's regional dodging, 0 off, 1 its own, 2 double.
Scanner LookLab Scan negatives: 0 is the neutral scan, 1 the minilab scanner's finish.
Red LightCinema print film only: the release printer's red light in printer points, 25 at the stock's aim. Each point is 0.025 log exposure; more red light adds cyan.
Green LightCinema print film only: the release printer's green light in printer points, 25 at the stock's aim. Each point is 0.025 log exposure; more green light adds magenta.
Printer PreflashPre-exposure of the print paper in relative log exposure units, softening highlights without lifting maximum density.
Blue LightCinema print film only: the release printer's blue light in printer points, 25 at the stock's aim. Each point is 0.025 log exposure; more blue light adds yellow.
Highlight DetailPrint the highlights darker, keeping their detail and colour: 0 off, 1 full. Not applied to the viewed negative or Cineon Log.
Shadow DetailPrint the shadows lighter, keeping their detail and colour: 0 off, 1 full. Not applied to the viewed negative or Cineon Log.
Negative ViewingHow the developed negative is read when Output Medium is Negative. Light Box sets the lamp so the clear film base sits just under white and keeps its orange. Scanner divides by the film base, so the base reads white. Other output media ignore this control.
StageWhich span of the pipeline this node performs. Full is the whole thing and is what every other setting here describes. Negative Only stops at the developed negative and writes its per-layer densities — data, not a picture — and Print Only takes exactly that back and finishes it on the selected medium, so the two in series reproduce Full. Texture Only lays the film's spatial character over the frame it is handed and leaves its colour alone. A Negative Only node must feed a Print Only node directly, with the same stock and lab settings and nothing in between: the densities are not colour and anything that grades, resamples or transforms them is not editing a picture.
Texture StagesWhether Texture Only carries this stage. Ignored by every other stage, where the strength controls below select what runs.
Render ModeDefault preserves the launch-time renderer setting. Realtime and Reference override it for this node, for both preview and delivery.

Swift library rendering

Embed FotufilmCore and FotufilmMetal via Swift Package Manager (see rendering-integration.md):

  • Checked rendering & memory budgets: Prefer throwing methods—FotufilmEngine.processChecked, developNegativeChecked, and printPositiveChecked—plus FilmEngineInvocation(validating:options:width:height:) so invalid buffers or unsupported push/pull steps surface as typed errors rather than fatal traps. Combined Legacy CPU rendering tiles automatically within a default 192 MiB intermediate budget (configurable via cpuMemoryBudget:).
  • Alpha & byte helpers: processSRGB8 and processDisplayP38 accept 3-channel RGB or premultiplied RGBA buffers and preserve coverage; floating-point planar APIs expect three finite unassociated planes of width × height samples.
  • Metal budget & kernel caching: Check HalideMetalFilmRenderer.minimumPeakBytes and canRender before dispatching large GPU frames. On macOS, SwiftPM clients automatically cache compiled Halide kernels by binary and toolchain hash without storing user images or edits.
  • Pack reloading: Call FilmStockPack.reload() to atomically invalidate cached pack generations across concurrent callers.
