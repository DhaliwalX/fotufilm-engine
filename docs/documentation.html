Fotufilm guide

Fotufilm adds a film look to photos and videos. This repository includes the engine, command-line tool, Mac app, Resolve and Final Cut plugins, and browser demo.

Build and open the app

Install Xcode 26 or newer and Halide. Run swift build to build the engine and swift test -c release --parallel to test it. Run macos/build.sh --test to build the Mac app.

Open build/macos/Fotufilm.app. Choose File → Open…, select a photo or video, and choose a film.

Film packs

The Mac film sidebar offers Film Workshop… and Load custom pack…. The pack purchase button is hidden.

Packs loaded in the Mac app are also available in the Fotufilm plugins on the same Mac and user account. Restart Resolve or Final Cut Pro after loading or updating a pack. Install matching app and plugin versions; older plugins skip packs that require a newer release.

The app and plugins include all 40 film profiles for free, including in default source builds. No activation is needed.

Thirty-three film profiles, including Gold 200, use smooth cubic interpolation through every validated digitized point from their source characteristic curves. This preserves small variations in the traced data and removes the old analytic clamp kinks. Points match within floating-point precision; they are not a claim of exact physical calibration. Beyond the source graph's exposure range, the response is extrapolated to constant density limits.

The native Metal camera renderer caches the cubic coefficients while preserving every sampled point. See the iPad performance measurements for timings and device limits.

These sampled profiles use stock schema version 2 and require an engine, app or plugin built with sampled-curve support. Older builds reject them. Existing schema version 1 profiles remain supported.

Scene light

Warmth and Tint change the light exposing the film. Warmer light means lower Kelvin; positive tint adds green light. Each stock retains its own fixed daylight or tungsten balance. Sources without capture-light metadata assume D65, so changing film does not change the assumed lamp.

Output calibration stays at the stock's reference light. Extreme tint shifts are limited to physically representable light.

RAW imports retain their complete as-shot white chromaticity. Temperature and tint edits happen after decoding and do not demosaic the file again. The camera normalizes acquisition at its declared scene white, then the engine applies that light once through the film's spectral sensitivities. A Kelvin value assumes a smooth Planckian/daylight spectrum; it does not identify an LED or fluorescent lamp.

The CLI --wb and --tint controls now change spectral scene light rather than digitally correcting RGB. For RAW, temperature is a relative mired adjustment to the capture light, with 6504 preserving it. In plugins, supply an image already balanced for its capture light; Scene Illuminant sets the base lamp and Temperature/Tint adjust it. Existing edits with non-neutral balance can render differently.

Developers can supply an exact capture xy white or an 81-sample spectrum covering 380–780 nm at 5 nm. A supplied spectrum takes precedence over the temperature/tint controls. Relative spectra are normalized to equal luminance, including lamps with no energy at 560 nm.

Print models

The output menu names each paper or print film: Kodak Ektacolor Edge, Kodak Professional Endura Premier, Fujicolor Crystal Archive Type CA, Kodak Vision 2383, Kodak Vision Premier 2393, and Fujifilm ETERNA-CP 3513DI. Each is digitised from the manufacturer's own published datasheet: Kodak E-7020, E-4070 and H-1-2383, Fujifilm AF3-0250U2, and the 2393 and ETERNA-CP 3513DI sheets. Dye spectra, layer sensitivities and characteristic curves are read from those publications rather than modelled. The film strip offers photo prints for still gauges and projection prints plus Telecine for motion-picture gauges; the film name does not change the choices. The CLI and tests also include synthetic films and a generated colour chart.

The CLI IDs are ektacolor-edge, endura-premier, crystal-archive, vision-2383, vision-2393, and eterna-cp. These identifiers are preserved, so saved print selections continue to resolve. Existing complete colour grades still load; incomplete grades are rejected.

Halation models

Halation Model offers Legacy and Layered Transport. Find it under Film Model in Mac and iOS settings, in the Halation group in Resolve and Final Cut, and beside Film stock in the browser. Legacy is the default for existing settings and projects. Layered Transport traces wavelength-dependent light through the film stack and uses an illustrative construction when a stock has no measured stack. Estimated Halation Shape applies only to Legacy.

Layered Transport is experimental, uses more memory and may render more slowly. Donor-layer stocks currently require Legacy. Browser transport uses SIMD WebAssembly and develops the whole frame; the browser stage walk remains available with Legacy. See the model guide for the illustrative A/B, construction fields, and backend limits.

Library rendering

When reusing an edit key, HandwrittenMetalFullFrameRenderer.prepareChecked keeps the previous edit usable if preparation fails. Frames already queued continue with the settings they were encoded with.

Scanned negatives

Choose File → Import Scanned Negative… and open a RAW or unconverted image with its clear film border visible. Drag over an unexposed border patch, choose the closest film, and use Preview Positive. Import Positive opens the result with Normal selected and the Crop tool ready.

Drag the four crop corners independently, then leave Crop or press Return to straighten the selected area. Corner edits support undo, saved edits and full-resolution export. Rotation and flipping keep the four-corner selection attached to the image. Drags use a cached display preview; film thumbnails refresh after leaving Crop. Choose an aspect ratio for a rectangular crop, or Reset Crop to start again.

Conversion is approximate and does not require a calibration wedge. Use unadjusted scans and their file colour profile; select Linear Samples only for a known linear scan. The positive retains full resolution in a 16-bit TIFF. Reimport the original to change border sampling or film choice. See the input requirements and conversion limits.

Developers can convert linear negative scans with the scanned-negative library API. Supply dark and light references plus a calibration mapping for the capture setup and film. The API recovers film densities and enters the existing print stage, preserving halation and grain already in the film. Measured scanner profiles and CLI controls are not included.

Plugins

Run resolve/build.sh --test or finalcut/build.sh --test to test a plugin. Building the installable Final Cut plugin also requires Apple's FxPlug SDK. See the Resolve guide and Final Cut guide.

Resolve controls

The inspector groups controls under Input, Film, Light & Colour, Lens & Filters, Development, Grain, Halation, Colour Separation, Output, and Pipeline, in the order the light passes through them. Input, Film, and Output start open. The Final Cut Pro plugin uses the same groups. Resolved Format, Resolved Medium, and Effective Renderer show the settings in use.

  • Film Frame Coverage (%) sets how much of the film frame you use.
  • Grain Animation follows the timeline or freezes the grain. Grain Seed lets you repeat a pattern.
  • Mottle changes coarse grain clumping. Return Spectrum adjusts halation across seven wavelength bands.
  • Render Mode selects Realtime or Reference. Disc grain needs a silver-image film and Reference mode.

Some controls depend on the film or stage you choose. For separate Negative Only and Print Only nodes, match their development settings, including Long Exposure (s).

Resolve and Final Cut fit finished prints to the timeline's colour gamut. Colours already inside it stay unchanged. This adjustment does not apply to density data or Texture Only output.

Film border and fringe effects

Adjacency changes development close to an image edge: the denser side gains a narrow border and the less-developed side loses density in a fringe. The command-line tool offers --adjacency-model screened-diffusion, an experimental density-dependent response with a two-Gaussian approximation to lateral diffusion with removal. It runs on the CPU and Metal renderers. Choose --adjacency-model gaussian for the original response; existing packs keep that default.

For an A/B, render the same source twice with the two model names and identical settings. Use --grain 0 --halation 0 to isolate the small edge changes. --couplers 0 disables adjacency together with inter-layer inhibition. The new model reuses the stock's radius and strength as starting values; it does not establish a new measured calibration for that stock. It models development edges, not optical halation or light leaks at the frame border.

Library clients can set FotufilmEngine.Options.adjacencyModel = .screenedDiffusion. A pack can author "adjacencyModel": "screened-diffusion". adjacencyRadiusMM retains its reference Gaussian-sigma units, with diffusion length equal to that value divided by √2. The new response acts on net formed density before reversal complementation and grain, and is bounded by the stock's development capacity.

Chromatic fringes

Fringe Amount in Film → Emulsion, beside Edge Contrast, spreads a fraction of inter-layer inhibition over a broader neighborhood. Its range is 0–100%; 0% preserves the original transport. Expand the arrow beside Fringe Amount to reveal Fringe Radius, which sets that broad Gaussian sigma in micrometers on the film, from 20–300 µm in the editor, with a starting radius of 100 µm. The radius must exceed the film's narrow inhibitor radius to act. Start with 5–20% and 50–150 µm; these are creative exploration settings, not a measured calibration for a film stock.

The fringe color follows the film's layer coupling and development curves. Uniform color patches stay unchanged apart from numerical rounding; neutral edges can acquire a tint. The existing Separation control also changes color throughout the image, while Edge Contrast changes inhibition within a layer. Monochrome films and films without inter-layer inhibition do not offer chromatic fringes. The effect does not model lens chromatic aberration or halation.

From the command line, use --fringe-amount 0.1 --fringe-radius 100; the radius is in micrometers. Use identical settings and --fringe-amount 0 for the baseline. Library clients can set FotufilmEngine.Options.chromaticFringeAmount and chromaticFringeRadiusMM (millimeters, so 100 µm is 0.1 mm). Nil uses the stock's values. Stock packs can author the same field names; absent fields mean amount 0 and radius 0.1 mm. The SDK accepts radius 0–2 mm and amount 0–1. Disabling couplers or the adjacency texture stage also disables the fringe. Existing packs and saved edits remain off by default.

Colour grading with the plugins

The engine decodes the image reaching the node into linear Rec.2020 light with mid-grey at 0.18, exposes the film with that light, develops it through characteristic curves, DIR couplers, halation and grain, and prints or scans the negative onto the selected Output Medium. The output is display-referred, with black and white set by the paper or scan, and is encoded back into the space that arrived at the node. Negative films deliver standard dynamic range; only reversal film on Digital Reference keeps light above display white. In a Rec.709 timeline the print is fitted into the Rec.709 gamut without changing luminance.

Log or linear wide-gamut timelines (DaVinci Wide Gamut / Intermediate, ACEScct, ACEScg or linear) are preferred over Rec.709 or sRGB, which clip at diffuse white and give halation no light above white. The Light & Colour controls (Exposure in stops, white balance, highlights, shadows, saturation, vibrance) act on the scene before the emulsion responds; a stop of Exposure moves the picture along the film's curve as in camera. Grading after the node regrades a finished print. Turn off host display transforms applied after the node, such as Resolve output tone mapping or an ACES output transform, or convert the print yourself to keep the paper's curve.

In an unmanaged DaVinci YRGB project Resolve labels the image Raw and the plugin assumes DaVinci Wide Gamut / Intermediate; add Color Space Transform nodes to DWG / Intermediate before the node and from it to the output space after, or set Timeline Color Space to the space that arrives. Colour-managed and ACES projects tag the image and Auto follows the tag. Final Cut Pro supplies linear light in the library's primaries, so Timeline Color Space selects primaries only. Use a Wide Gamut HDR library for log or HDR sources and place exposure and balance corrections above Fotufilm in the Video inspector.
