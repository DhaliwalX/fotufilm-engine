Fotufilm guide

Fotufilm adds a film look to photos and videos. This repository includes the engine, command-line tool, Mac app, Resolve and Final Cut plugins, and browser demo.

Build and open the app

Install Xcode 26 or newer and Halide. Run swift build to build the engine and swift test -c release --parallel to test it. Run macos/build.sh --test to build the Mac app.

Open build/macos/Fotufilm.app. Choose File → Open…, select a photo or video, and choose a film.

Film packs

The Mac film sidebar offers Film Workshop… and Load custom pack…. The pack purchase button is hidden.

Packs loaded in the Mac app are also available in the Fotufilm plugins on the same Mac and user account. Restart Resolve or Final Cut Pro after loading or updating a pack. Install matching app and plugin versions; older plugins skip packs that require a newer release.

The app and plugins include all 40 film profiles for free, including in default source builds. No activation is needed.

Gold 200 uses smooth, monotonic characteristic curves fitted to Kodak E-7022 (February 2016), page 4. This removes a kink near the blue channel's shadow threshold and softens abrupt highlight flattening. The response beyond the published exposure range is a smooth extrapolation.

Print models

The output menu names each paper or print film: Kodak Ektacolor Edge, Kodak Professional Endura Premier, Fujicolor Crystal Archive Type CA, Kodak Vision 2383, Kodak Vision Premier 2393, and Fujifilm ETERNA-CP 3513DI. The source distribution supplies analytic example receivers under these material names. Their descriptions identify this limitation; the names alone do not establish measured calibration. The film strip offers photo prints for still gauges and projection prints plus Telecine for motion-picture gauges; the film name does not change the choices. The CLI and tests also include synthetic films and a generated colour chart.

The CLI IDs are ektacolor-edge, endura-premier, crystal-archive, vision-2383, vision-2393, and eterna-cp. These identifiers are preserved, so saved print selections continue to resolve. Existing complete colour grades still load; incomplete grades are rejected.

Plugins

Run resolve/build.sh --test or finalcut/build.sh --test to test a plugin. Building the installable Final Cut plugin also requires Apple's FxPlug SDK. See the Resolve guide and Final Cut guide.

Resolve controls

The inspector groups controls under Input, Film, Light & Colour, Lens & Filters, Development, Grain, Halation, Colour Separation, Output, and Pipeline, in the order the light passes through them. Input, Film, and Output start open. The Final Cut Pro plugin uses the same groups. Resolved Format, Resolved Medium, and Effective Renderer show the settings in use.

  • Film Frame Coverage (%) sets how much of the film frame you use.
  • Grain Animation follows the timeline or freezes the grain. Grain Seed lets you repeat a pattern.
  • Mottle changes coarse grain clumping. Return Spectrum adjusts halation across seven wavelength bands.
  • Render Mode selects Realtime or Reference. Disc grain needs a silver-image film and Reference mode.

Some controls depend on the film or stage you choose. For separate Negative Only and Print Only nodes, match their development settings, including Long Exposure (s).

Resolve and Final Cut fit finished prints to the timeline's colour gamut. Colours already inside it stay unchanged. This adjustment does not apply to density data or Texture Only output.

Colour grading with the plugins

The engine decodes the image reaching the node into linear Rec.2020 light with mid-grey at 0.18, exposes the film with that light, develops it through characteristic curves, DIR couplers, halation and grain, and prints or scans the negative onto the selected Output Medium. The output is display-referred, with black and white set by the paper or scan, and is encoded back into the space that arrived at the node. Negative films deliver standard dynamic range; only reversal film on Digital Reference keeps light above display white. In a Rec.709 timeline the print is fitted into the Rec.709 gamut without changing luminance.

Log or linear wide-gamut timelines (DaVinci Wide Gamut / Intermediate, ACEScct, ACEScg or linear) are preferred over Rec.709 or sRGB, which clip at diffuse white and give halation no light above white. The Light & Colour controls (Exposure in stops, white balance, highlights, shadows, saturation, vibrance) act on the scene before the emulsion responds; a stop of Exposure moves the picture along the film's curve as in camera. Grading after the node regrades a finished print. Turn off host display transforms applied after the node, such as Resolve output tone mapping or an ACES output transform, or convert the print yourself to keep the paper's curve.

In an unmanaged DaVinci YRGB project Resolve labels the image Raw and the plugin assumes DaVinci Wide Gamut / Intermediate; add Color Space Transform nodes to DWG / Intermediate before the node and from it to the output space after, or set Timeline Color Space to the space that arrives. Colour-managed and ACES projects tag the image and Auto follows the tag. Final Cut Pro supplies linear light in the library's primaries, so Timeline Color Space selects primaries only. Use a Wide Gamut HDR library for log or HDR sources and place exposure and balance corrections above Fotufilm in the Video inspector.
